## The LoopHole Project — the format (fully verified, theloopholeproject.ai/rules)

A non-commercial global collaborative AI film: **177 creators, 193 approved films**, one continuous Community Film. Every contribution follows the same four-beat grammar, and every film ends where the next creator's world begins:

1. **FALL** — an original character falls into the scene from above and lands in a new world.
2. **THREAT** — the character discovers an original creature, danger, or unexpected presence.
3. **RUN** — the character turns and runs to escape.
4. **JUMP** — the character jumps into a clearly visible hole, portal, or passage and disappears — into the next creator's universe.

Everything else is the creator's: character, threat, world, visual style, camera, rhythm, **AI tools and production workflow**, sound design. AI generation may be combined with editing, compositing, animation, 3D, practical footage — any technique. Hard constraints: one submission per creator; **max 20 seconds**; 16:9 master (9:16 optional extra), min 720p, recommended 1080p, MP4/MOV H.264; sound design required; optional ≤1s of sound over black at head/tail to smooth audio transitions; **no titles, credits, logos, or end cards inside the video** (continuity of the chain film); **original worlds only** — no existing fictional characters, no recognizable real people, no brands/logos, no copyrighted assets.

Kurupuri's 20-second segment at 03:05 sits between Mystique Licorne and TechHalla — their world is one link in the chain.

That is a handoff discipline — design the head and tail of a clip as universal joints, keep the middle entirely your own. Kurupuri's segment satisfies it; so do all 193 approved films.

## Liftable techniques for WuBu MV builds

1. **THE LOOPHOLE (banked FORM idea).** The Fall–Threat–Run–Jump chain grammar as a transition system for montage builds: a scene (or a whole bumper) that *begins with an arrival* (something falls/drops/enters from above into the world) and *ends with an exit through a visible portal* (hole, door, screen, mouth, drain) that the next scene's world sits behind. In MV terms: portal-chained cuts where the exit of take N is the entrance of take N+1 — stronger than our last-frame→first-frame chaining because the joint is a designed EVENT (fall in / jump out), not just a matched frame. For a 10s bumper: fall in on the downbeat, one threat/transformation event, run, jump out on the final hit — the loop closes back to the opening fall, making the bumper literally loopable.
2. **Universal-joint heads and tails.** Prompt/design every take with a standard entry state and exit state (arrival-from-above; exit-through-portal) so takes become interchangeable links — any take can follow any take. This directly serves the CONTINUITY method and replacement-pack repairs: a replacement take only has to satisfy the joint contract, not match a specific neighbour.
3. **Metamorphosis as the event.** Per the (lead-level) signature: sculpture-like figures and unexpected transformations. In event-doctrine terms, TRANSFORMATION is the family our own ledger shows underused (MV-12/13/14 all spent the same two mechanics); Kurupuri's whole practice is a reminder to spend transformation events — a figure becoming a statue, an object becoming a creature — as the scene's one event, especially on instrumental/bridge slots where no mouth is owed.
4. **The hell-portal artist frame, as a look discipline.** "Generative AI is a link to hell. I reached in and grabbed what I could." — treat each generation as retrieving something from a coherent elsewhere: one world-logic per video, everything in it "grabbed" from the same place. A cheap coherence device for montage builds: name the elsewhere in the scene plan (the world the portal opens onto) and let every take be a grab from it.
5. **No in-clip titles/credits (LoopHole rule) as chain hygiene.** Already WuBu law for generated text; the LoopHole version adds the reason: any burned-in title breaks the chain for the NEXT segment. When building sequences meant to be re-cut or chained (bumpers, episode atoms), keep all type in the post layer — which is already our kinetic-type rule, now with a second source.
6. **The gag-transformation (manzai timing).** From a comedy writer: structure a transformation slot like a joke — mundane setup held just long enough to read as normal, then the turn, then the morph as the punchline, cut on the laugh point. Our event doctrine already bans bland events; this adds the TIMING shape for transformation events specifically: normal → wrong → transformed, with the cut landing on the transformation's peak, not its settle.
7. **Sketch-then-compile volume discipline.** Kurupuri posts raw 10s bursts and later curates them into Motion Sketches volumes. WuBu's bumper factory already produces atoms; the liftable is the explicit second pass — after a run of bumpers, compile a "volume" cut (a 2-minute sketch-reel of the best bursts) as its own deliverable, rather than treating atoms as only episode feedstock.
