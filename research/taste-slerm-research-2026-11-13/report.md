## 0. The diagnosis (one paragraph)

Our bland work is not under-detailed — it is *over-even*. The editing stream's headline: our rejections were never gate failures, they were taste failures the gates couldn't see. MV-5 passed every gate and the user called it "the weakest"; MV-11 passed every gate and was "slop but boring." The visuals stream's one-sentence diagnosis: bland takes are centred subject, flat light from nowhere, everything sharp, no foreground, no grain, no reason for the camera to move — "the gap is not missing detail, it is missing error" (Versely). A frame lands when everything in it has a *physical cause* — a lens, a light source, a foreground, a texture, a motivated move. And a cut lands when it serves emotion first and arithmetic second (Murch: emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm only 10% — which is why a master can pass every timing gate and still feel dead).

## 1. Ranked top-10 taste upgrades (with the exact skill/assembly change each implies)

Each entry names the Slopcannon stage or assembly note it changes. Parent applies; nothing here is yet in the skill.

### #1 — Rule-of-Six feel triage: fail a master on emotion, not just on numbers
**Source:** Walter Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye* (Rule of Six: emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10%, eye-trace 7%, 2D plane 5%, 3D space 4%) — https://blogs.ischool.berkeley.edu/i290-viznarr-s12/the-rule-of-six-walter-murch/ ; https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/when-and-where-to-make-the-cut-inspired-by-walter-murchs-in-the-blink-of-an-eye/ ; Karen Pearlman, *Cutting Rhythms* — "rhythm is time, energy, and movement shaped by timing, pacing, and trajectory phrasing, for the purpose of creating cycles of tension and release" — https://blog.routledge.com/humanities-and-media-arts/cutting-rhythms-creative-film-editing/
**Fixes:** MV-5 v4 ("gates passed, master still wrong — weakest"); MV-11 rebuild ("approved as slop but boring"); MV-8 v2. This is the ONLY upgrade that addresses the class "the user rejects a gate-passing master."
**Exact change — Stage 5 (QC), G6:** add a triage read no numeric gate can substitute. (a) At master QC, score three questions per section 1–5: does this section *feel* like its lyric (emotion)? does the picture change the story (new information per cut)? does the rhythm breathe (tension→release, not flat)? (b) A master where all technical gates pass but any section scores ≤2 on emotion FAILS G6 — named failure mode "MV-5 class," not a surprise at user playback. (c) The plan must name, per section, the tension (what tightens) and the release (what lets go) — a section with no named tension/release is an unplanned section. Needs a named human-or-agent reader at Stage 5 triage, not a script.

### #2 — Per-section energy arc: density follows the song, measured at plan time
**Source:** Hanna Eng, *How to edit a music video* ("cut to the beat… shorten the cuts progressively… chorus/drop: cut hardest, on the beat") — https://www.hanna-eng.com/guides/monter-un-clip-musical/ ; David Bordwell via Cinemetrics (classical ASL 8–11s → modern 4–6s; action tier 2–3s, *Mad Max: Fury Road* 2.66s) — http://cinemetrics.uchicago.edu/article/5dcb488e-0f5c-4a5c-824b-a052518861ba ; Mellius — https://www.melliusai.com/edit-music-video ("cutting everything at maximum density flattens the song's dynamics"); Mixider — https://mixider.com/blog/how-to-cut-video-on-the-beat (at 120 BPM a cut every beat is a shot change every 0.5s, "frantic").
**Fixes:** MV-12 bridge density 40.5/min vs the 66/min target (now a pass/fail number at plan, not a disclosed nit at delivery); MV-11's flat single-density flow; MV-2's cuts sitting on the 10s grid (uniform grid = no energy arc at all).
**Exact change — Stage 3 (Plan) + Stage 6 (Assembly density gate):** plan carries a per-section ASL table, in cuts/minute: verse 12–20/min (ASL 3–5s, shots 2–4s); pre-chorus ramp 20→40/min (2 bars → 1 bar → 1 beat); chorus/final chorus 40–66/min (ASL 0.9–1.5s, 1–2s/shot, 2–4 cuts per bar max); bridge/breakdown 15–25/min (release, ≥30% below the preceding chorus — a texture break: location, colour, speed, or one unbroken take, never just "more of the same faster"); outro 10–15/min (one long wide hold). Final chorus must be the densest section in the master (≥1.5× verse density). Never cut on every beat for a whole section — vary on-beat/off-beat; hold a shot across bars to build tension, then snap back to on-beat cutting when energy lifts.

### #3 — Singer-cut discipline: look → cut → reaction; the mouth-lie is a gate fail
**Source:** Lev Kuleshov (1910s–20s experiments: same neutral face reads as hunger/grief/lust depending on the cutaway) via https://artlist.io/blog/kuleshov-effect-or-how-an-editor-shapes-and-strengthens-an-actors-performance/ ; Sven Pape / This Guy Edits ("use reaction shots liberally; cut out weakness; a reaction taken from the start of a scene can be reused at the end") via https://blog.musicvine.com/every-edit-matters/ ; Hitchcock's extension (cut back to a *smiling* reaction) via https://iamandrewellis.medium.com/the-editors-effect-32bd7a3a510f
**Fixes:** MV-5, the single biggest open wound — "bad timing, cat talking when she is" (Q10: L104 closed-mouth, S16 chorus-2 inter-lines L58/L60/L62 on cover). This would have caught v4 at plan time instead of at user playback.
**Exact change — Stage 6 + WORD-SYNC mouth gate + Stage 5 G6:** (a) A lyrical cut to a singer is legal only if that singer's mouth reads open/transitional at onset *and* +0.15s (already the gate) **and** the outgoing shot ends on a look, a breath, or the *other* singer's reaction — never a hard cut from one open mouth to another open mouth mid-phrase. (b) Inside a sung phrase, the only legal singer change is at a phrase boundary; a mid-phrase singer change is a GATE FAIL, not a style choice. (c) For alternating lines (MV-5 bridge, GIRL-first), the plan must write the look/reaction shot into the vehicle as its own slot — the listener's face is a planned shot, not assembly salvage. A mouth-lie cut (singer change mid-phrase, or cut to a closed/wrong mouth under a sung line) fails G6 as a named class.

### #4 — The 0–3s hook: cold open on the peak image, first cut ≤2.5s
**Source:** our own Performance Analysis 2026-11-12 (first 3 seconds decide it: ST-066 32.5% skip vs ST-042 87.5% skip, same day, same format, 27× views apart; 10s bumpers the only format that ever performed); https://kompozy.io/how-to/optimize-first-3-seconds ("cut the runway… make it frame one — a cold open"); https://thrivenvision.com/short-form-video-retention ("start at the most interesting moment and backfill afterwards"); https://www.capcut.com/create/short-form-video-hooks-first-3-second-patterns (hook 0–3s, promise 3–5s).
**Fixes:** MV-5 "low grab for song intro" — the exact user words; MV-11 v1's "same image then 4 seconds of splash" opening shape.
**Exact change — Stage 3 banger-test gate (strengthened):** replace "hook in the first 0.5–3s" with: (a) frame 0 is the song's peak image (the carry element + a motion event + an open mouth if the intro is sung) — no leader, no establishing still, no cloned pre-word frames; (b) first cut lands at ≤2.5s, on the first downbeat or first word onset, whichever is first; (c) the intro's first 3 seconds are mute-tested at QC: motion visible + carry element visible + (if sung) mouth open. A plan whose S01 vehicle describes a settle, a zoom-in, or "the scene opens" fails the Stage 3 gate. Note: a 3-minute master is now watched like a feed item — the first 3 seconds are the thumbnail.

### #5 — The physical-camera LOOK block: name the hardware, not the vibe
**Source:** Versely, "Grain, flare and vignette against the AI sheen" — https://www.versely.studio/blog/grain-flare-and-vignette-against-the-ai-sheen ; invideo, "AI Video Prompts in a Cinematographer's Style" — https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-write-an-ai-video-prompt-in-the-style-of-a/ ; PromptPat, "How to Write Cinematic AI Image Prompts" — https://www.promptpat.com/blog/how-to-write-cinematic-ai-image-prompts/ (frame = "something is mid-action, the light comes from a source the scene could hold, the camera sits somewhere on purpose, the palette was chosen, and the air is visible").
**Fixes:** MV-11's flatness; MV-4's hue/skin drift (no light source named = the model invents one per take); MV-2's volley-18 restages coming back photorealistic (no lens/grade lock stated); MV-5's weak intro grab.
**Exact change — vehicle template (every vehicle, fixed order after the action text):**
```
[at <place> action text…]
Shot on 35mm film, [28mm|50mm|85mm] lens, [shallow depth of field, background dissolving | deep focus, sharp foreground to horizon].
Light: [one named source, e.g. warm practical lamp at frame left], subject at 45 degrees to the source, [soft bounce fill from camera-right | no fill, deep shadows].
Grade: [palette: max 2 primaries + bone + ink], [warm | cool] cast, lifted blacks.
Camera: [one move, see #9].
Texture: visible fine 35mm grain, slight falloff toward the corners.
16:9, exactly 10 seconds.
```
Focal-length defaults: 35mm journeys/montage, 50mm performance, 85mm singing close-ups. Vague adjectives ("cinematic", "moody", "atmospheric") are banned as the *only* style language. Keep the LOOK block ≤40 words (lab battery used 45–80-word video texts; long-prompt behaviour untested). Our tool has no negative-prompt field — exclusions must be written as positive stable states (see #10), never as "no X".

### #6 — Three-layer depth in every shot + frame-within-a-frame
**Source:** iFilmThings, "What Is Shot Composition? 9 Rules and 8 Films That Use Them" — https://ifilmthings.com/shot-composition-in-film/ ("Add a foreground: a shoulder, a door frame…"; Roma "fills its frames from front to back, so its heroine is always one person inside a bigger world"); Backstage, "Shot Composition in Film" — https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/shot-composition-film-guide-12842/ ; Imaginode, "Composition Guide" — https://imaginode.ai/en/cinematic-techniques/composition
**Fixes:** MV-11 (one bowl, one duck, one room, one vantage — zero layering for 172s); MV-12 stage/office stills attracting signage (empty backgrounds invite the model to invent signs — a named foreground/background fills the space with *intended* content); MV-13 S42 tail settle (a layered frame keeps reading even when the subject holds still).
**Exact change — shot-design rule (Stage 3 plan review):** every vehicle names all three layers explicitly: `Foreground: [object/texture within ~1m of lens, slightly soft or framing the edge] — Midground: [subject + action] — Background: [world, with one readable event or light change]`. A vehicle naming fewer than two layers is rejected at plan review. Frame-within-a-frame at least once per 10s beat in montage sections (doorway, arch, window, crowd gap, carry-object silhouette). Lead room + thirds, not centre: subject's eyes on a thirds intersection, never dead centre except as a deliberate formal/symmetrical beat (centre = confrontational/formal — only when the lyric is a confrontation or a title moment); horizon on the top or bottom thirds line, never mid-frame. Negative space is a placement, not an accident: declare the empty area for type in the ref brief (anabology: every plate prompt declares an empty area for type — e.g. "left third empty dark wall for kinetic type"). Numbers: foreground 5–15% of frame area at an edge/corner; subject at 1/3 or 2/3 horizontal; background ≥1 distinct depth cue (overlap, scale contrast, or haze).

### #7 — Cut on action: every joint lands mid-motion, with a matched exit/entry
**Source:** cut grammar as codified in StudioBinder *Shot List* Ep. 10, summarised at https://filmmakergenius.com/academy/film-editing-workflow/basic-film-editing-techniques ("Match on action… the smoothest cut there is"); Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye* (Rule of Six, eye-trace) via https://blogs.ischool.berkeley.edu/i290-viznarr-s12/the-rule-of-six-walter-murch/ ; match-cut taxonomy at https://get-spark.io/blog/how-to-find-match-cuts-and-creative-transitions (2001 bone→spacecraft; *The Graduate* upward-movement chain; Wright's matching transitions).
**Fixes:** MV-11 eruption lags (S03/S09 crowns erupt 1–2s after "Water" because the chain frame is calm water — cut *into* the eruption mid-motion instead of chaining from calm); MV-13 S55 shoulder pat landing 3.3s beyond the slot (the pat is a motion event — the window should be placed on the motion, not the take); MV-11 stop-start flow generally.
**Exact change — extend the CONTINUITY METHOD joint check (Stage 6) from "framing/lighting/subject position match" to a *motion* match:** (a) every cut lands 2–4 frames *before* a motion peak inside the outgoing take, never on a settle; (b) the incoming take's first 6 frames must contain motion in the same screen direction or a matched shape/position (graphic match), verified by frame-diff + eye read; (c) a joint where outgoing motion is ~0 (settle) is a QC fail even if the frames match statically. Vehicle-side: every vehicle names its exit motion ("ends mid-reach / mid-turn / mid-crest") so assembly has motion to cut on.

### #8 — Come late, leave early: the head/tail trim law
**Source:** Sven Pape / This Guy Edits (tips 5–6, "come late, leave early" and "cut out the process") via https://artlist.io/blog/kuleshov-effect-or-how-an-editor-shapes-and-strengthens-an-actors-performance/ and https://blog.musicvine.com/every-edit-matters/ ("Performance is not a performance until the editor shapes it"; Pudovkin: emotional content comes more from editing than from the actor).
**Fixes:** MV-10 systematic mouth-lag (6 onsets, Q8 — currently gated behind a parent-ordered re-fire volley; this rule makes half of it fixable in reassembly); MV-5 "low grab" intro (S01 leader: 40 cloned pre-word frames — sanctioned then, now trimmed); MV-11 S42 tail settle / S20 duck-ride head.
**Exact change — Stage 6 assembly, head/tail trim law:** (a) No take enters the cut with >0.5s of pre-action settle: scene in-point = latest of (first frame with frame-diff above the scene's live baseline, first mouth event −0.25s). (b) Scene out-point = the last motion frame + ≤0.3s; a tail settle longer than 12 frames (0.5s) is trimmed, not shipped. (c) Where a take's mouth lags its own audio (MV-10 class, 0.5–1.4s), trim the lag out and place the mouth event on the song onset — do not warp the lag into place, and do not ship the lag as a disclosed nit. If trimming the lag breaks the warp caps, the slot goes to montage (bad-word rule), not to a stretched mouth. **Companion rule — picture leads the beat (J-cut analogue):** at every *section* boundary, the incoming shot's first frame lands 2–6 frames (0.08–0.25s) before the downbeat — picture anticipates the beat; at every *phrase* end, the outgoing singer's reaction holds 0.3–0.5s into the next phrase before the cut. Never stack the picture cut exactly on the audio event at a section boundary; a cut landing exactly on the downbeat reads as a block join. Verify on the master: section-boundary cuts sit in [−6f, −2f] relative to the downbeat. Sources: https://cursa.app/en/article/j-cuts-and-l-cuts-how-audio-transitions-make-your-edits-invisible ; https://filmmakergenius.com/academy/film-editing-workflow/basic-film-editing-techniques

### #9 — One motivated light + colour discipline: light is a section device
**Source (light):** LightzOut Studios, "Cinematic Lighting Techniques" — https://lightzoutstudios.com/cinematic-lighting-techniques/ (contrast-ratio ladder; "You're not just illuminating a subject. You're carving them out of darkness"); Seedance.tv, "10 Cinematography Lighting Techniques to Master" — https://www.seedance.tv/blog/cinematography-lighting-techniques ("Don't settle for prompts that only say 'cinematic lighting'… Describe direction, contrast, source motivation, colour, and atmosphere"; warns crushing everything into shadow hides the eyes); Canon, "Lighting techniques for video" — https://www.canon.co.za/pro/stories/using-lighting-video/ (motivated lighting "needs a logical reason to be there").
**Source (colour):** Medisurvey, "How Color Theory Influences Viewer's Emotions in Music Videos" — https://medisurvey.com/articles/how-color-theory-influences-viewers-emotions-in-music-videos ; J. Scalco, "Mastering Music Video Color Grading" — https://www.jscalco.com/music-video-color-grading/ (90% of first-90-seconds judgement is colour; one-look-per-track: Beyoncé "Hold Up" golden, Weeknd "Blinding Lights" neon, Billie Eilish desaturated blue); anabology "Escape Velocity" recipe — https://aimv.gg/v/anabology--suno-song-12-hour-opus-5-5-run-with-a and https://claudia.gallery/record/escape-velocity/readme/ (every plate prompt ends in the same palette/light terms — "blue-black and steel blue, one small red status lamp as the only warm light"; v1 "way too gray" fixed by a colour-halftone print pass; "keep generation inputs in colour, apply the look in post").
**Fixes:** MV-4 hue drift (S10/S16/S17/S25 blue-violet skin — sourceless light lets the model re-invent skin tone per take); the general flat "AI sheen"; MV-11 monotony (no colour arc for 172s); MV-12 era worlds (the logo carry works *because* each era has a distinct palette — formalise it); MV-13 v1's grey/muted reads.
**Exact change — vehicle template + scene plan:** (a) Every vehicle names **exactly one key source + its in-frame motivation + direction + contrast**, e.g. `Key: low warm sun through the window at frame left, 45° to subject, 8:1 chiaroscuro, no fill, rim light from the neon sign behind separates her from the wall.` Never write "cinematic lighting" alone. Contrast ratios: ~2:1–4:1 soft/flat for comedy/pop brightness; **8:1 or higher = dramatic chiaroscuro**. **Eyes rule:** the key must leave both eyes readable — if the contrast choice would hide an eye, the vehicle is wrong. For singing takes, Rembrandt or loop lighting is the default; full chiaroscuro is for montage/non-singing slots only. (b) **Palette line in every vehicle + every ref brief:** `Palette: [2 primaries max] + bone + ink; accent [colour] on ≤10% of frame area (one prop / one lamp / one garment detail only).` This extends the station palette law from type into *footage*. One warm accent rule: exactly one small warm light/object per shot when the palette is cool, and vice versa. (c) **Section colour map in the scene plan:** one line per section — verse cool/desaturated, chorus warm/saturated, bridge colour-shift (not set-shift). Colour temperature follows the section's emotion: warm (2000–3500K) intimacy/nostalgia; cool (5000–10000K) detachment/modernity/tension — state the Kelvin band when the section's emotion turns. Light as the scene change (SoundBoost, already banked): "42 scenes in ONE house, light changes instead of location (blue present / golden memory)". (d) Never generate grey/monochrome refs or takes: colour lives in the ref, the grade lives in post; a desaturated take is *rescuable* (recolour from its own ref still — Deep Exemplar shape), but a grey ref poisons the take, so add a colour check to ref QC: ref must contain the palette's accent colour visibly.

### #10 — Silhouette-first casting + stage around the model's failure modes

**Fixes:** MV-5 wrong-character mouthing (cat vs girl must read in black, not just in colour); MV-2's wrong-face scenes (a silhouette-distinct night-VOICE would have failed QC at the still stage, not at the master); MV-7's hobo-vs-otaku identity drift; MV-12's Clippy reading as "a white edge mark at still scale" (failed the thumbnail test); MV-12's MOUTH/CROWD lesson; MV-5's CAT fangs on singing beats; MV-4's blue-violet skin.
**Exact change — casting + vehicle + assembly post pass:** (a) **Casting-call stills before any volley:** for each character, generate 3 silhouette-distinct directions as stills first (turnaround + one action pose + face sheet), pick by thumbnail readability at ~120px wide. (b) **Silhouette test on every ref still before it enters a vehicle:** threshold the ref to solid black, view at thumbnail size — if the character/prop isn't identifiable, re-generate the ref. Add to volley QC as a check. (c) **Vehicle pose line:** every performance vehicle states the pose as a *silhouette sentence*: `Pose: [one verb + one shape, e.g. "arms thrown wide in a V, head back, coat flaring — silhouette reads as a star"]`, limbs separated from the torso (elbow out, gap between legs, head clear of shoulder line). No "standing and singing" — a standing figure's silhouette is a rectangle. Written canon beats ref image for identity: the character's invariant traits are restated in *every* vehicle text as a fixed block, never paraphrased per take. **Pose-as-cut:** at each 10s boundary, the *pose* changes while the world changes around it — the cut hides in the pose change. (d) **Staging bans (vehicle-level, checkable):** no open hand mid-gesture toward camera; no fingers spread; no hand gripping + turning + face acting in the same take; no rapid expression change in a singing take; no extreme close-up of a face in motion (medium close-up is the safe floor); no two hard problems in one take (face acting + hand interaction + camera move — pick one hard problem, stage the rest easy). **Positive-phrased stability block** appended to every character vehicle: `Same face, same proportions, same [canon traits] throughout. Hands relaxed, fingers together, at rest or holding [one named object]. Locked camera, lighting and background unchanged.` (e) **Backs-to-camera is a feature:** MV-14's crowd-backs-only staging is the house precedent — backs, profiles, hands-on-objects, over-the-shoulder are first-class coverage for any slot where the face would be the risk. A mouth that can't be trusted is never shown; the song carries the line via kinetic type. (f) **Post texture pass (assembly step):** after grade/match, apply fine monochrome grain weighted to midtones/shadows (invisible on a still at normal viewing distance — "only obvious when you toggle it off and the frame suddenly looks plastic"), a fraction-of-a-stop vignette centred on the optical centre, saturation −10–15%, blacks lifted slightly. Same grain profile on *every* clip in the cut. Judge grain on the final export at phone size, never on a 480p preview. Grain costs bitrate — keep it light for the ≤50 MB chat copy. Portrait/close-up vehicles add `skin pores and stubble visible, uneven skin tone, fine lines, soft natural light from one source`.

### A.1 c11_superflux — SuperFlux onset detection + harmonic-gated flux (TOP-1)
**What it is.** Böck & Widmer's SuperFlux (DAFx 2013): log-frequency spectrogram → 3-bin maximum filter along frequency (tracks spectral trajectories, kills vibrato-induced false positives) → half-wave-rectified spectral flux. Published claim: up to **60% fewer false positives** without missing events, on 25,927 mixed-audio onsets. Second ODF in the same sprint: rectified **complex-domain / weighted phase-deviation** (Duxbury et al. 2003/2004; Dixon 2006 "Onset Detection Revisited") — phase carries timing; a drum transient and a vocal onset differ in phase behaviour. Third variant, the actual hypothesis: **harmonic-gated flux** — flux computed only on HPSS harmonic magnitudes (masks corr 0.999229 vs librosa, already built) or only near pYIN F0 harmonics (mix pYIN corr 1.0000, already built), so drum/bass peaks never enter the function. This is the new signal class loop 6 asked for by name.
**Sources:** SuperFlux paper — http://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Boeck_Widmer_DAFx_2013.pdf (Böck & Widmer, DAFx-13). Hop 2: Dixon's comparison — https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2006/dafx.pdf ; librosa `onset_strength` implements the same local-max-filter idea and cites Böck & Widmer 2013 — the in-venv validation reference (librosa is already installed). Hop 3: chirp-group-delay onset — https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13734v1 (claims parity with SuperFlux at 300% efficiency — follow-up only, single paper, no MIREX track record).
**Weakness fixed:** (a) stay-rule sensor — the #1 open problem; also (e) onset grid (sharper than plain flux for the snapper/beat front end).

## 3. Stream B — MV editing taste (full findings)

**Headline:** our catalogue's rejections were never gate failures — they were *taste* failures the gates couldn't see. The missing layer is a *feel* rubric (Murch's Rule of Six) plus density/energy discipline per song section. Hop chains: Murch (*In the Blink of an Eye*) → PremiumBeat/Artlist explainers → StudioBinder *Shot List* Ep. 10 (Rule of Six, eye-trace, J/L-cuts) → cut grammar; Tony Zhou / Every Frame a Painting → Jackie Chan's 9 principles → Edgar Wright visual comedy → Gondry/Jonze/Romanek (MTV directors list, 1992 credits) → Goodwin's *Dancing in the Distraction Factory*; Kuleshov → Sven Pape / This Guy Edits → Karen Pearlman *Cutting Rhythms* → tension/release; Bordwell/Salt ASL data → Cutting/DeLong/Brunick → MTV-generation blame (Postman 1985).

### B.1 Cut on the energy arc, not the metronome — per-section ASL targets
Shot length is a dial turned with the song's energy: long in verses, progressively shorter through the pre-chorus, shortest at chorus/drop, opened back up in the bridge/breakdown so the viewer breathes before the next peak. Cutting on *every* beat for a whole song is mechanical; pros mix on-beat, off-beat, and held shots, and reserve the fastest cutting for the payoff. → **Top-10 #2** (full rule + sources there).

### B.2 Cut on action — every joint lands mid-motion, with a matched exit/entry
Cut in the middle of a movement so the motion carries the eye across the join and the cut disappears. The outgoing and incoming shots share a motion vector, a shape, or a screen position (graphic match cut). Murch's eye-trace (Rule of Six #4, 7%): the viewer's focus must land where the next shot needs it. → **Top-10 #7**.

### B.3 Singer-cut discipline: look → cut → reaction — never cut to a mouth that isn't singing
The Kuleshov/Hitchcock pattern — show the character looking, cut to what they're looking at, cut back to their reaction. Meaning (and, for us, *who is singing*) is created by the juxtaposition, not by either shot alone. The editor, not the performer, decides who the audience believes is singing. → **Top-10 #3**.

### B.4 Come late, leave early — cut the process, keep the event
Enter every shot at the latest possible moment and leave at the earliest — cut the walk-in, the breath, the settle, the wind-up. "Cut out the process": audiences can see what's going on; descriptive lead-in is fluff. Shots are building blocks, not finished performances — the editor shapes the performance by removing what doesn't work. → **Top-10 #8**.

### B.5 The 0–3s hook is the whole test — cold open on the peak image, first cut ≤2.5s
The viewer decides keep-or-swipe in ~2–3 seconds. Cut the runway: no greeting, no logo sting, no slow zoom-in, no establishing still. Frame one is the most arresting moment, already in motion; change something (angle, scale, graphic, text) every 2–3 seconds thereafter. Mute-test: if the first 3 seconds muted don't say what the video is, the hook has failed. → **Top-10 #4**.

### B.6 Collision montage — build sequences from contrast, not from coverage
Eisenstein's five methods, in ascending order — metric (cut by length alone), rhythmic (length + movement inside the shot), tonal (emotional tone/light/shape), overtonal (all three combined), intellectual (two colliding shots produce a third meaning present in neither). Montage is not sticking pieces together; it is the *collision* between neighbouring fragments. Pudovkin's linkage (smooth continuity) is the opposite pole — use linkage inside a sung phrase, collision everywhere else.
**Sources:** Sergei Eisenstein, *Film Form* / *The Film Sense* ("Word and Image," 1929) via https://media-studies.com/eisenstein-montage/ and https://storyfirstmedia.com/content-over-aesthetic/ ; lecture-note synthesis at https://fiveable.me/film-history-and-form/unit-10
**Rule (folds into Top-10 #2 and Stage 5 G6):** formalise the existing "montage density is a QC dimension" (MV-11 verdict) into Stage 5 G6 as a counted rule. (a) Every montage sequence (any non-sung span ≥5s) must contain ≥3 distinct collision types per 10s, chosen from: scale change (wide↔close), angle change, world/location change, graphic match, action match, light/tone flip. (b) No single vantage >10s without a cutaway — already Q9's audit bar, now a Stage 5 gate, not a queue item. (c) Bridges get the Wright/Cameron "tool-up montage" treatment (Tony Zhou's Edgar Wright essay, point 6: action synchronised to music; close-ups of hands/objects cut to the beat): a bridge is a rapid object/action montage on the beat grid, never a held wide. (d) Metric-only cutting (uniform shot lengths regardless of content) is a named fail — it is Eisenstein's *lowest* method, and it is what a uniform-grid plan produces.
**Fixes:** MV-11 single-subject flow (one bowl, one duck, one room, 172s); MV-8 v2 (singing character at centre + reused footage — a montage with no collisions is just a long take); MV-12 bridge density (technique B.1 gives the number, this gives the content).

### B.7 Rule-of-Six QC — judge every cut by emotion first, and fail the master on feel
→ **Top-10 #1** (full rule + sources there).

### B.8 Picture leads the beat by 2–6 frames — the MV analogue of the J-cut
In dialogue film, a J-cut lets the next scene's audio arrive before its picture, and an L-cut lets the old audio linger over the new picture — the ear leads or trails the eye, and the join disappears. In our pipeline the master audio is fixed, so the *picture* does the leading/lagging: the new world's first frame lands a few frames *before* the downbeat (picture anticipates the beat), and a singer's reaction holds a few frames *past* the phrase end. → **Top-10 #8, companion rule**.

### B.9 The 3 bland editing anti-patterns
**B-a. The held talking-head / single-vantage master.** One singer, one room, one angle, held for the whole song. Tony Zhou's line on lazy comedy applies verbatim: "These movies aren't movies. They're lightly edited improv." *Examples:* MV-11 master #2 (user: "repetitive," "same image with splash"; rebuild: "approved as slop but boring, single-subject — one bowl, one duck, one room for 172s"); MV-8 v2 (singing character at centre + reused footage). *Cure:* Top-10 #2 + B.6 — per-section ASL targets and the ≥3-collisions-per-10s montage rule. A master with one vantage held >10s is now a G6 fail, not a disclosed nit.
**B-b. Metronome cutting — density divorced from energy.** Cutting on every beat at a constant rate (Eisenstein's metric montage, his *lowest* method), or the mirror image: a uniform grid regardless of the song (MV-2's cuts on the 10s grid, 1.5–2.5s off true boundaries). Both read as arithmetic, not music; the viewer feels the grid, not the song. *Cure:* Top-10 #2 — the energy arc, with density *measured* per section at plan time. Trap in the other direction: Goodwin (*Dancing in the Distraction Factory*, 1992) observed that fast-cut montage renders images "impossible to grasp on first viewing, thus ensuring multiple viewing" — speed is a tool for rewatchability, not a substitute for a legible singer's mouth. Fast in the chorus, legible in the verse.
**B-c. The mouth-lie cut.** Cutting to a closed mouth — or the wrong singer's mouth — under a sung line. Kuleshov in reverse: the juxtaposition *creates* a false performance, and the audience's brain registers the lie even when every timestamp is right. *Examples:* MV-5 v2/v3 (cat talking when the girl sings; user verdict 2026-11-13); MV-5 L104 on a closed-mouth passage; MV-10's six lagged onsets; MV-13 S25's first "What?!" landing on a closed mouth. *Cure:* Top-10 #3 — singer changes only at phrase boundaries, via a look/reaction, with the mouth gate enforced at onset +0.15s. A mouth-lie is now a named GATE FAIL class in Stage 5, not an ear item discovered after delivery.

### B.10 Proposed exact skill edits (consolidated — parent applies)
1. **Stage 3 (Plan), banger-test gate** — replace "hook in the first 0.5–3 s" with: "Frame 0 is the peak image (carry element + motion event + open mouth if sung); first cut ≤2.5s; intro passes a muted 3s test. Plan carries a per-section ASL table (verse 12–20/min, pre-chorus ramp 20→40, chorus 40–66, bridge 15–25, final chorus densest at ≥1.5× verse). Plan names per-section tension and release (Pearlman)."
2. **Stage 5 (QC), G6** — add counted rules: "no single vantage >10s without a cutaway; montage spans ≥5s contain ≥3 collision types per 10s (scale/angle/world/graphic-match/action-match/tone-flip); metric-only (uniform-length) cutting fails G6; mouth-lie cut (singer change mid-phrase, or cut to a closed/wrong mouth under a sung line) fails G6; Rule-of-Six triage read per section (emotion/story/rhythm 1–5) — any section ≤2 on emotion fails G6 even if all numeric gates pass."
3. **Stage 6 (Assembly)** — add: "Head/tail trim law: in-point = latest of (first motion frame, first mouth event −0.25s); no >0.5s pre-action settle and no >12f tail settle enters the cut. Section-boundary cuts land 2–6 frames before the downbeat; phrase-end reactions hold 0.3–0.5s into the next phrase. Every joint passes a motion match (outgoing cut 2–4f before a motion peak; incoming first 6f carry motion in the same direction or a matched shape)."
4. **WORD-SYNC section** — append to the singer-cut rule: "Inside a sung phrase, the only legal singer change is at a phrase boundary, reached via a look/reaction shot. Alternating-line bridges write the listener's reaction as its own planned slot."

### C.6 Section-energy cut grammar — verses breathe, choruses cut, bridges break texture

### C.7 One motivated move per take — and the morph is a transition, not an accident
Every camera move means something: push-in = "this matters" (intimacy/realisation), pull-out = isolation/reveal, tracking = journey/immersion, handheld = urgency/panic, locked-off = intensity (stillness traps the character and lets performance carry the frame), whip pan = shock/transition (the blur hides a cut). A move with no story reason is empty style within seconds. Crucially for our tool: models have no persistent 3D model of the scene, so long/compound moves accumulate error — one simple move per take survives; compound moves drift.
**Sources:** Aelia Vision, "Camera Movement Guide" — https://aeliavision.com/camera-movement-guide/ (motivated-movement table; "if the movement has no story reason, it quickly becomes empty style"); Joey Arcisz, "Camera Movements: The Language of Motion" — https://www.joeyarcisz.com/blog/camera-movements-the-language-of-motion ("Every movement is a word. Every combination is a sentence"; static = deliberate trapping; push-in = universal "this matters"); SuperPrompts, "Why Your AI Video Prompts Look Like Stock Footage" — https://app.superprompts.pro/blog/ai-video-camera-movement-prompts (camera-line structure: `[shot size] [move] [direction/speed], [what the move reveals or follows], [lens feel]`; "describe the move, then the reason"); Zooop, "Camera Movements and What Each Move Does" — https://zooop.ai/glossary/camera/camera-movements (dolly = parallax, "what makes a dolly feel like real space"; whip pan blur hides a cut; "models generate a few seconds at a time and have no persistent 3D model… long or compound moves accumulate error").
**Concrete rule (vehicle template, folds into Top-10 #5's Camera line):** one move per vehicle, stated as move + reason + reveal: `Camera: slow push-in over 10s, closing on her face as she realises — reveals the [carry object] glowing behind her, 50mm.` No vehicle names two moves. No "slow zoom" (our eyes can't zoom — it reads as unnatural; a push-in is the honest version). Move vocabulary per slot type: singing close-up = **locked-off** or **slow push-in only** (any other move fights the mouth); journey/montage = tracking or pull-out; hook hit = push-in landing on the downbeat; transition = whip pan (blur hides the cut — pairs with the two-image morph); bridge/outro = crane-up or pull-out reveal. **Two-image morph discipline (video-api lab):** the morph happens at 0.5–1.0s and the middle is invented — so *stage the morph as the cut*: the carry object (CLIP/tablet/feather/sailboat) crosses the lens, passes behind the subject, or the whip-pan blur covers 0.5–1.0s. Never ask the morph to be invisible; make it the transition device. End-anchor is the least exact frame (MAE 51.85 vs 43.17) — cut *on* the landing in assembly, never hold on it. **Locked-off is a choice, not a default:** a locked-off take must have *internal* motion (subject moves, light changes, crowd crosses) — a locked camera + still subject is the halted-photo failure, not a style.
**Fixes:** MV-11's halted-photo-then-movement starts (the move must already be happening at the cut point); MV-10's take-head mouth-lag (locked-off singing takes with the first word ≥1.5s in — pre-roll staging — is the R3 vehicle fix); MV-13's S42 tail settle and S55 late pat; the two-image morph mush in early journey takes (morph unstaged = visible melt).
