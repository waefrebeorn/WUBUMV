## Ranked list — dismissed tools and their slermable cores

Ranked by value for Slopcannon's core on this blade (1.5 shared CPUs, 6 GB RAM, Python 3.12, no GPU). Each entry: what it is, where it was dismissed, the slermable core, C11 feasibility, validation bar, what it replaces or enables.

### 1. C11 voicing gate — YIN + pYIN + CPP + HNR ★ TOP 1 — sprint scope in SPRINT_SCOPE.md

- **What it is:** a per-frame voicing decision for sung audio: F0, voicing strength, cepstral peak prominence, harmonics-to-noise ratio — four mutually-checking measures of "a voice is sounding here, at this pitch".
- **Where dismissed:** three places at once. (a) Survey §3 model-bound list: madmom RNN+DBN / BeatNet CRNN — the beat trackers whose shells gave us sprint 3. (b) Sprint 6 rec #3: "a C11 pitch/voicing detector (YIN)" as the next candidate after the HPSS boundary gate failed. (c) Loop 1's seed, which named this exact sprint and its bar.
- **The slermable core:** four classical algorithms, all fully specified in papers, none needing weights:
  - **YIN** (de Cheveigné & Kawahara, JASA 2002): autocorrelation → cumulative-mean-normalized difference function → absolute threshold (0.1) → parabolic interpolation. Gross error **1.03%** on speech with laryngograph ground truth — ~3× lower than the best competing methods of its day. No upper frequency limit, which is what makes it good for singing. FFT-based autocorrelation, O(N log N).
  - **pYIN** (Mauch & Dixon, ICASSP 2014): YIN run at multiple thresholds, candidate F0s with probabilities from a Beta prior, HMM/Viterbi smoothing over time. The probabilistic shell around YIN; "YIN is shown to be state of the art" on singing voice. librosa.pyin and Essentia PitchYinProbabilistic are the reference implementations to validate against.
  - **CPP — cepstral peak prominence** (Hillenbrand et al. 1994; Hillenbrand & Houde 1996 smoothed CPP): peak of the cepstrum above a regression line, in dB. The most robust acoustic correlate of dysphonia severity (Maryn et al. 2009 meta-analysis); ASHA 2018 recommends CPP as the general measure of dysphonia, *replacing* jitter/shimmer/HNR. The property that matters here: CPP works on connected speech **without pitch tracking** — it does not require F0 first — and it integrates noise + periodicity into one number. Cutoffs are software-specific (Praat: 14.45 dB sustained /a/, 9.33 dB running speech; Hillenbrand CPPS ≥ 4.0 normal) — calibrate on MV tracks, do not import a clinic's threshold.
  - **HNR — harmonics-to-noise ratio** (Boersma, IFA Proceedings 1993): the height of the autocorrelation maximum at the pitch lag, HNR = 10·log10(r(τmax)/(1−r(τmax))). "Several orders of magnitude more accurate than the methods commonly used for speech analysis" — the lag-domain formulation with parabolic peak interpolation and window correction is the whole trick. Healthy sustained voice ~20 dB. Efficient variant: Hanning window, 6 glottal cycles (mean HNR error < 0.42 dB).
- **C11 feasibility: YES — the easiest sprint on this list.** FFT already exists in c11_mir (lego it in, do not rewrite). Autocorrelation, cepstrum (FFT → log → IFFT), parabolic interpolation, and a Viterbi smoother (snapper's DP machinery, reused) are all scalar C11. Estimated runtime: YIN + CPP + HNR on a 161.6 s track at 16 kHz, 10 ms hop ≈ 16k frames × small FFTs — well under 5 s. Memory: one frame + one FFT buffer at a time.
- **Validation bar (from loop 1's seed, tightened):** ≥80% of vocal-activity boundaries within ±0.5 s of hand-checked MV-14 boundaries (sung-span starts/ends marked by ear on the Demucs vocal stem); YIN F0 vs librosa.pyin on MV-14, corr ≥ 0.99, gross error ≤ 2%; CPP/HNR separate sung spans from instrumental spans (median sung CPP ≥ instrumental CPP + 3 dB); and the gate that matters: vocal-activity boundaries from the voicing gate must beat HPSS's 12.5% starts / 8.3% ends — bar **≥80% starts within ±0.5 s**. If it clears that bar, Demucs stops being the segmenter for boundary work (Demucs stays the *separation* reference).
- **Replaces / enables:** replaces the HPSS boundary gate (sprint 6's failed half); enables the snapper's owed "stay if no in-span strong peak" rule (the voicing curve *is* the in-span evidence); enables loop 1's unsung flag (a lyric-sheet line with no voicing under it is unsung, not mis-timed); enables loop 1's mouth-open-vs-quiet gate (MAR × vocal RMS needs a vocal-activity curve, and RMS alone is what failed in sprint 6); feeds the piecewise grid (voicing onsets are section-agnostic anchors).

### 2. Birdsong syllable splitter — Sound Analysis Pro's classical pipeline

- **What it is:** Sound Analysis Pro (SA+, Tchernichovski et al. 2000) converts continuous song into discrete syllables and extracts per-syllable features — duration, pitch, pitch goodness, entropy — by amplitude-threshold segmentation on the spectrogram. SongSeq (Daou et al. 2012) adds syntax scores: linearity, consistency, stereotypy, KL distance, sequence entropy. Chipper (Searfoss et al. 2020) is the open-source semi-automated version: threshold → segment → measure frequency/duration/syntax, for birdsong *and other natural sounds*.
- **Where dismissed:** the neural version — TweetyNet (Cohen et al. 2022, a single network that segments spectrograms into annotated syllables) — is what the modern literature reaches for, and it is model-bound. The classical SA+ pipeline underneath it was never evaluated; the survey never listed birdsong at all. Loop 1's seed named this cross explicitly.
- **The slermable core:** amplitude-threshold segmentation on a log-mel or STFT magnitude spectrogram (the same threshold-and-cluster move the sprint-6 diagnosis used for pixel diffs, applied to sound); per-segment feature vector (duration, median pitch via YIN, pitch goodness = YIN CMNDF depth, spectral entropy); segment-boundary agreement as the validation. No training, no weights, no species-specific anything — a sung phrase is a sung phrase.
- **C11 feasibility: YES.** Threshold segmentation is ~50 lines on top of c11_mir's STFT. Spectral entropy is a histogram over the magnitude spectrum. Pitch goodness falls out of the YIN implementation from #1 for free.
- **Validation bar:** syllable boundaries agree with the snapper's word boundaries on MV-14 within ±0.1 s on ≥70% of words (the two methods are independent — spectrogram-energy vs onset-flux — so agreement is real evidence); entropy separates sung syllables from drum hits / breaths (median sung entropy < percussive entropy, p < 0.01 on MV-14's 212 words). Failure mode to watch: melisma (one word, many notes) splits into multiple syllables — that is *correct* behavior for a syllable splitter and must be merged by the word grid, not counted as error.
- **Replaces / enables:** a second, independent segmentation of the vocal line to cross-check the snapper (which currently has exactly one flux flavour and a known early-snap failure mode); an entropy feature for the "is this a note or noise" decision in QC; a syntax/consistency score for repeated choruses (SongSeq's linearity — are the three choruses actually the same shape?).

### 3. CCA audio-visual synchrony — SyncNet's classical core

- **What it is:** canonical correlation analysis between audio features (envelope / MFCC / modulation spectrum) and visual features (mouth-region motion / landmark trajectories), with a lag search. The correlation peaks at the true AV offset; the peak's height is the sync score.
- **Where dismissed:** F-01 SyncNet — dismissed as a trained model (two-stream CNN + contrastive loss). The survey kept it model-bound. But SyncNet's *job* — measure whether mouth motion and audio agree, and by how much — predates it by decades: Hotelling's CCA (1936); Weenink's Praat implementation (SVD-based, 2003); Xie et al. (Interspeech 2012) used CCA to align audio-visual feature sequences for pattern discovery; Sargın et al. (ICASSP 2006) used CCA explicitly for "synchronization of audiovisual" streams; Pedersen et al. (2022) showed CCA-derived temporal modulation filters: face motion correlates with the acoustic envelope below 10 Hz, peaking at the 3–4 Hz syllable rate — peri-oral movement *is* the speech rhythm.
- **The slermable core:** CCA = generalized eigenvalue problem on two covariance matrices, solved by SVD — ~100 lines of C11 on top of the existing FFT/MFCC machinery, plus the C11 Farneback flow (sprint 7) for the visual motion feature. No weights. The lag search (±500 ms, 10 ms steps) is the sync measurement; the peak correlation is the score.
- **C11 feasibility: YES, with one honest caveat.** CCA itself is trivial. The visual feature needs a mouth region per frame — which needs the face box from #5 (Viola-Jones) or the InsightFace ONNX detector (sprint 5's #5 plan, adopt-as-is). CCA is sprintable the moment the face box exists; sequence it after #5, or prototype the audio side first against hand-marked mouth boxes.
- **Validation bar:** the CCA sync score must discriminate MV-8 v2 (rejected: singing character, wrong sync) from MV-8 v3 (accepted) the way TSM M6 did — 4× separation on the same pair is the bar to beat, 2× is the bar to pass; per-take lag estimate within ±100 ms of the hand-checked lag on 5 MV-14 takes with visible mouths. If it cannot separate v2/v3, it is a measurement, not a gate — still useful, but say so.
- **Replaces / enables:** a SyncNet-free LSE-C/LSE-D (SyncNet's two outputs are exactly "is it in sync" and "how far off" — CCA's peak height and peak lag are the same two numbers, classically); the quantitative half of loop 1's improvement #3 (asymmetric tolerance needs a *measured* lag, not a guessed one); a take-level sync score for volley QC that does not need a GPU or a weights file.

### 4. MSAF's classical structure — Foote + CBM + OLDA + 2D-Fourier

- **What it is:** music structure analysis — automatic verse/chorus/bridge boundaries from audio. MSAF (Nieto & Bello, ISMIR 2016) bundles eight algorithms; four are classical and C11-re-creatable.
- **Where dismissed:** F-15 MSAF — the survey's verdict was "adopt, don't SLERM: pip install msaf". That is correct for *using* MSAF, and it misses the point for *learning from* it: the algorithms inside are classical, and the pipeline needs their outputs as first-class data (section boundaries feed the piecewise grid, loop 1's #4), not as a pip dependency's side effect.
- **The slermable core:**
  - **Foote's checkerboard kernel** (Foote & Cooper 2003): build a self-similarity matrix from chroma/MFCC, correlate a checkerboard kernel along the diagonal → novelty curve → peak-pick boundaries. ~80 lines on top of c11_mir's chroma.
  - **CBM — correlation block-matching** (Serrà et al. 2014): frame boundary retrieval as global DP maximizing block-structure score around the diagonal. The 2026 unsupervised evaluation found CBM the most effective of the three downstream methods tested. DP machinery already exists (sprint 3/5).
  - **OLDA — ordinal linear discriminant analysis** (McFee & Ellis 2014): learn a projection that separates nearby frames from distant ones. The *supervised* part needs labels; the classical residue is plain LDA — a generalized eigenvalue problem, same SVD machinery as CCA (#3). Unsupervised variant: use the Foote novelty peaks as weak labels.
  - **2D-Fourier magnitude coefficients:** the SSM is invariant to the representation; 2D-FFT of the SSM captures repetition structure. FFT exists already.
- **C11 feasibility: YES.** Foote first (simplest, most cited), CBM second (best performer, DP reuse), OLDA third (shares SVD code with #3 — build #3 and #4's OLDA together). SSM on a 161.6 s track at 1 fps chroma = 162×162 — trivial.
- **Validation bar:** boundaries within ±0.5 s of the hand-marked MV-14 sections (Intro 0.00 / V1 14.14 / C1 36.44 / V2 55.90 / C2 77.78 / Bridge 98.50 / Final 112.16 / Outro 128.20 — 8 boundaries, 7 interior) on ≥6 of 7; same test on MV-13 and MV-12 (their section maps are in their PREPRO files). A boundary the algorithm finds that the hand map missed is investigated, not auto-counted as error — the hand map is a claim, per the audio-analysis rule.
- **Replaces / enables:** hand-marked section boundaries at intake (the current method — a human reading a dadaspec plot); the fences for loop 1's monotonic forward walk (an alignment path that crosses a section fence with the wrong occurrence count is rejected — the fences have to come from somewhere, and audio-derived fences beat hand-marked ones for consistency); the piecewise grid's section list (loop 1's #4 needs per-section phase, which needs per-section boundaries, which is this).

### 5. Viola-Jones Haar cascade — MediaPipe Face Mesh's classical front-end

- **What it is:** face detection in 15 fps on a 700 MHz Pentium III (2001): integral image (summed-area table — any rectangle sum in 4 lookups) + Haar-like features (edge/line/four-rectangle contrast templates) + AdaBoost feature selection (~6,000 features from 160,000+ in a 24×24 window) + attentional cascade (38 stages; background windows die at stage 1–2).
- **Where dismissed:** F-03 MediaPipe Face Mesh / MAR — dismissed as a landmark net (model-bound; sprint 5's #5 plan is the InsightFace ONNX shell, adopt-as-is). But the MAR gate does not need 468 landmarks. It needs a *mouth region*, and a mouth region needs a *face box*, and a face box is exactly what Viola-Jones solved classically.
- **The slermable core:** the integral image + Haar feature evaluation + cascade evaluation are pure classical code (~300 lines). The honest line: the *cascade itself* is trained weights — AdaBoost selected the features on labeled faces. Re-creating the training is not the sprint; *running* the published cascade is. OpenCV's `haarcascade_frontalface_default.xml` is the trained artifact — reuse it the way the pipeline reuses a Whisper weights file, and be honest that the weights are adopted, the *algorithm* is slermed. (Same honesty standard as whisper.cpp: the code is ours, the weights are adopted.)
- **C11 feasibility: YES — trivially.** The whole detector at 160×90 QC resolution is a few thousand rectangle evaluations per frame. The one dependency: a frame reader — which is the lego top-3 build c11_frames (not yet built; sequence after it, or prototype against ffmpeg-extracted PGM frames).
- **Validation bar:** face-box IoU ≥ 0.7 vs InsightFace buffalo_l detections on 20 MV-14 takes with visible faces; false-positive rate < 5% on frames with no face (backs-to-camera crowd shots — the common case); runtime < 50 ms/frame at 160×90 on this blade. Mouth-region proxy (lower third of the face box) must contain the mouth in ≥90% of hand-checked frames — if it does not, the MAR gate needs the landmark net after all, and this entry's verdict flips to "front-end only".
- **Replaces / enables:** the face-presence check in QC (currently an eye-read); the mouth-region supplier for the MAR × RMS gate (loop 1's #3) and for CCA's visual feature (#3); a cheap "is there a face at all" gate for montage slots (the mouth rule is about *prominent open mouths* — you cannot check it if you cannot find the face).

### 6. Sakoe-Chiba DTW forced alignment — Qwen3-ForcedAligner's classical core

- **What it is:** dynamic time warping (Sakoe & Chiba, IEEE TASSP 1978): optimal alignment of two feature sequences by DP with slope constraints, symmetric form superior. aeneas (the forced-alignment tool) is DTW over MFCCs: synthesize the text with TTS, align the TTS MFCCs to the recorded MFCCs, read off word times. A 2023 CTC-DTW paper reports mean alignment error 50 ms for speech, 120 ms for singing — median below 50 ms for both.
- **Where dismissed:** Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B — dismissed as a trained model (the lyrics report's path (3), "if lyric sheet known, re-time with Qwen3-ForcedAligner"). MFA (Kaldi GMM-HMM) is the proven fallback (20 ms average deviation from hand labels). Both are models. The *alignment step* underneath both is DP — and this pipeline already owns three DP implementations (beat DP, snapper Viterbi, CBM).
- **The slermable core:** DTW over MFCC sequences with a Sakoe-Chiba band and slope constraint. The aeneas variant needs a TTS reference — no TTS on the blade, and Bark/Chatterbox are out by standing law — so the loop-2 variant is different: DTW the *Whisper cross-attention* word posteriors (already computed) against the lyric sheet's word sequence, or DTW the snapper's onset-flux curve against a synthetic "expected onset" curve built from the lyric sheet's syllable counts. Either way the DP is the same code as the snapper's, with a different cost matrix.
- **C11 feasibility: YES.** MFCC exists in c11_mir. DTW with a Sakoe-Chiba band is O(N·band) — trivial. The honest limit: DTW aligns *sequences*, it does not decide *what was sung* — garbage in (wrong lyric sheet) gives confident garbage out. The lyric sheet is ground truth by order (loop 1's #1), so this is acceptable.
- **Validation bar:** median |offset| vs MFA word times on MV-14 ≤ 50 ms; ≥80% of words within ±100 ms; no word placed outside its section fence (loop 1's monotonic-walk guard, enforced in the DP). If DTW cannot beat the snapper's current median |correction| (0.052 s), it is a cross-check, not a replacement — and a cross-check that disagrees with the snapper on >10% of words is itself a finding (one of them is wrong; the hand-check decides).
- **Replaces / enables:** a second, independent word grid to cross-check the snapper (loop 1's #1 says forced-align the known sheet — this is the *classical* way to do that sentence, no Qwen3 weights needed); the monotonic forward walk for repeated choruses (DTW with section fences enforces it by construction).

### 7. TransNetV2's classical shell — frame similarity + color histograms

- **What it is:** shot-boundary detection. TransNetV2 (Souček & Lokoč, ACM MM 2020; arXiv:2008.04838) is a 3D-CNN with stacked dilated convolutions, dual output heads, and two *optional classical modules*: a frame-similarity module and a color-histograms module. The paper itself notes traditional algorithms "provided results deemed as sufficient approximations at some popular benchmarks" (Smeaton et al. 2010); Hassanien et al. (2017) used color-histogram differencing as post-processing to suppress false positives.
- **Where dismissed:** F-10 TransNetV2 — model-bound; the survey's substitute was "TSM M5 + NMS". That substitute is right for *scene* boundaries in the assembled master. It misses the *inside-a-take* job: the generator's ~0.5 s morph point in two-image takes, and the hard cut the generator sometimes hides mid-take.
- **The slermable core:** the two optional modules, minus the network: per-frame color-histogram difference + frame-similarity curve (cosine on downsampled frames) + non-maximum suppression on the combined novelty curve. All classical, all scalar, all computable from ffmpeg-extracted frames at 160×90.
- **C11 feasibility: YES — after c11_frames.** Histogram difference is ~30 lines; the frame reader is the gating dependency (lego top-3 build, not yet built).
- **Validation bar:** detect the known morph point in 5 two-image MV-14 takes within ±0.2 s (the morph is at ~0.5 s by the video-api-mastery finding); zero false boundaries on 5 single-image takes' steady regions. If histogram difference fires on every lighting change, add the frame-similarity term and re-test — the two modules exist precisely because one alone over-fires.
- **Replaces / enables:** the "where does this take actually change" measurement for QC (currently an eye-read on contact sheets); a cut-energy curve to pair with sprint 7's flow_curve.py (which measures motion energy — this measures *content* change; the two curves together say whether a cut is a real cut or a morph).

### 8. Essentia arousal/valence, classical core — Thayer's 2D model

- **What it is:** arousal (energy) and valence (positiveness) from audio. Essentia's arousal/valence models are trained regressors; the *classical* model underneath is Thayer (1989): arousal ≈ energy + tempo, valence ≈ mode (major/minor) + spectral brightness.
- **Where dismissed:** F-11 Essentia energy/arousal — the survey's substitute was "#1 band loudness". That substitute covers arousal's energy half and drops valence entirely.
- **The slermable core:** arousal = per-beat RMS + band loudness + tempo (all exist in c11_mir — per-beat band loudness is a validated feature, corr 1.000000 vs librosa); valence = mode estimate from the chroma vector (major vs minor template correlation — Krumhansl-Schmuckler key profiles, 1990, classical) + spectral centroid. No training.
- **C11 feasibility: YES — almost free.** Every input feature already exists in c11_mir. The build is a small mode-estimation function + a curve writer.
- **Validation bar:** arousal curve correlates with the measured RMS curve on MV-14 at r ≥ 0.9 (sanity — it should, it is built from it); valence separates the MV-14 bridge (minor-key, low) from the final chorus (major-key, high) by ≥0.3 on a −1..1 scale, matching the hand-marked section map. If valence does not separate them, the chroma templates are wrong for this material — say so and drop valence, keep arousal.
- **Replaces / enables:** the per-section energy curve for cut-density planning — SoundBoost's corroborated rule ("cut density follows measured loudness") needs a *curve*, and arousal is the named version of it; a valence curve as a second opinion on section boundaries (chorus should read happier than verse — if it does not, either the boundary or the mode estimate is wrong).

### 13. Lego Tier-3 — verdict: three LLM-only, one meta-tool
