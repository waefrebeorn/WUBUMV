# Esoteric AI Video & Attention — How Surreal / Body-Horror Shorts Stop the Scroll

## Summary

The scene the producer is pointing at is real, identifiable, and larger than one artist. Its centre of gravity is **not horror filmmaking in the traditional sense, but a feed-native practice: a mundane, instantly readable scene (office, subway, pool, hallway, family portrait) violated in the first second by one impossible, fleshy or scale-wrong event, then looped or cut before the brain resolves it.**

- **Anchor case — verified live:** Kurupuri (@kurupuri0, X) had **15.5K followers and 19.8K posts on 15 Nov 2026** (research-window read). The profile banner itself is the technique in one still: two ordinary office workers in shirts and ties pressed against giant fleshy, multi-eyed, open-mouthed faces filling an office. Recent posts in the live feed were caption-less, with individual posts at a few thousand views and a pinned “Little Red Riding Hood” post at 55,434 views. Source: https://x.com/kurupuri0 — *verified live*.
- **The LoopHole Project is the best single map of the network.** Its official site, premiered 6 Nov 2026, lists a Community Film built from **177 creators / 193 approved films**, every segment following the same 4-beat loop — **Fall → Threat (a creature/danger) → Run → Jump into a hole/portal** — with each film ending where the next creator’s world begins, max 20 seconds per segment. Kurupuri is in the roster in order of appearance, alongside TechHalla and ~175 others. Sources: https://theloopholeproject.ai and https://www.theloopholeproject.ai/rules — *index*.
- **Beyond that roster, the names with documented surreal/grotesque signatures and documented tools are:** niceaunties (Auntieverse), Gossip Goblin / Zack London (mutated-creature sci-fi epics, 1M+ Instagram), Kelly Boesch (surreal music-video shorts, 2M+ reported followers), Another World / @anotherworldcore (dreamcore/liminal, self-reported 2.3M combined followers), Paul Trillo (Sora morph music videos), shy kids (Sora “Air Head” balloon-man), The Dor Brothers (viral AI satire/action, harvesting workflow), Dave Clark, Akihiro Miyagi / AI_MIYAGI and Miho Kinomura in Japan, plus festival-circuit work (Runway AIFF, Curious Refuge AI Horror Competition).
- **Workflow signal is consistent across otherwise very different creators:** still image first (Midjourney / DALL·E / Flux), then image-to-video animation chosen per effect (Runway, Kling, Hailuo/MiniMax, Pika, Luma, Veo, Sora), then heavy selection and post — 25–30 prompts per niceaunties video, 50 generations → ~2 keepers for Dor Brothers, hundreds of generations at a 300:1 ratio for shy kids’ Sora film. ComfyUI + AnimateDiff + custom LoRA is the documented open-source route for seamless morph-loops and for deliberately keeping flicker/deformation.
- **Attention science supports the mechanism, but does not directly prove it for AI video:** disgust images hold attention at 100–200 ms in lab tasks; scene-inconsistent objects are fixated earlier/longer and remembered better (prediction error); choosing morbid images activates reward circuitry in fMRI; trait morbid curiosity predicted over half the variance in choosing to investigate morbid stimuli. Platform-side, TikTok’s own ad-creative guidance (widely reprinted) says 63% of highest-CTR videos put the key message in the first 3 seconds — that is ad data, not organic proof. **No study found directly tests grotesque AI clips vs. retention in a TikTok/Reels feed — flag that gap to the producer.**

Confidence: high for creator identities, LoopHole structure, and named tool stacks (primary or creator-quoted sources); medium for audience sizes (several are self- or PR-reported); the attention explanation is a well-sourced extrapolation, not a direct test.

### 2. Visual techniques that recur — and concrete examples

These are ordered by how directly they operate in the first 0–3 seconds.

**1. Start already transformed — no “before” shot.**
The hook is a *state*, not an event the viewer waits for. Kurupuri’s banner/first frames are already flesh-fused office workers (verified live). shy kids’ *Air Head* opens on a man who simply *has* a balloon head. Trillo’s *Abstract* (MIT Technology Review description) has a figure morph into a glitter ball and a “breakdancing trash man” as raw Sora output. Producer translation: frame 1 should be unexplainable; explanation, if any, comes later or never.
Sources: https://x.com/kurupuri0 (*verified live*); https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/03/28/1090266/how-three-filmmakers-created-soras-latest-jaw-dropping-videos/1214 (*index*).

**2. One flesh/organic invasion of a mundane, legible space.**
Office (Kurupuri), subway (*The Commute — NYC Subway Mutants*), prison (*JAILBIRD*, animal POV inside a human institution), domestic labour (*Goddess*). The background must read instantly — desks, fluorescent light, commute — so the violation reads instantly too. Gossip Goblin’s *Patchwright* pushes the same move to flesh-**and-metal** hybrids in a Blade Runner-like bunker world.
Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EvcHdebL7KY ; https://www.europesays.com/us/678341/ — *index*.

**3. Morph / transformation as the clip’s only event (10-second form).**
Face-borrowing in *Trick or Trend*; creature transformation in Runway Merit film *Emergence*; body-collapse in *Awful Dreams* (“bodies shift unnaturally, forms blur and collapse”). In ComfyUI culture this is literalised as a workflow: AnimateDiff morph workflows blend **four still images into one continuous morph loop** (IPAdapter + ControlNet), i.e. the transformation never resolves into a stable object.
Sources: https://curiousrefuge.com/ai-film-gallery/trick-or-trend-ai-horror-short ; https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/awful-dreams ; https://finechat.ai/blog-ComfyUI-Master-Morphing-Videos-with-PlugandPlay-AnimateDiff-Workflow-Tutorial-25699 — *index*.

**4. Scale violation + impossible geometry, with no creature at all (dreamcore branch).**
Another World’s own published “Infinite Fall” prompt is the most concrete artefact found — verbatim excerpt from the creator’s site: *“an aerial view of a highway interchange that forms a perfect infinite loop… the scale is wrong making the roads seem both massive and miniature, dreamcore surrealism — --chaos 100”* (Midjourney V8.1). That single Reel is credited on the same page with 64.5M Instagram + 38.5M TikTok views. Kapwing’s dreamcore guide generalises the recipe: a familiar setting (backyard, school hallway, swimming pool) *“but something is slightly off,”* generated as a still, then animated.
Sources: https://www.anotherworldcore.com/ ; https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-make-dreamcore-videos-with-ai-prompts-and-step-by-step-guide/ — *index*.

**5. Loop structures that deny resolution.**
- LoopHole’s mandated relay: Fall → Threat → Run → Jump-into-hole, ≤20 sec, end = next world’s entrance. The jump/portal is a cut that feels like continuity.
- Seamless morph loops (AnimateDiff “closed loop”: match first and last frame) and curated micro-loops (the 7.667-sec Slop Art portrait loop above).
- Trillo’s *Washed Out* video inverts it: instead of looping, a **single continuous fly-through/zoom** morphs one life stage/environment into the next (55 Sora clips edited as one move), so there is never a cut at which to scroll away.
Sources: https://www.theloopholeproject.ai/rules ; https://apatero.com/blog/comfyui-animation-workflow-video-generation-2026 ; https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/paul-trillo-ai-films-weird-cameras/ — *index*.

**6. Texture wrongness as a deliberate finish — grain, VHS, flicker.**
Dor Brothers’ early signature is described (via a Lindenwood University study of the studio) as “gritty realism… early CCTV or VHS footage,” 16mm/8mm/black-and-white grain — originally a way to make model errors unreadable, later a recognisable look. At the art end, the Cuerpa Mostra creator (first-person project page) deliberately used SD 1.5 + AnimateDiff + a custom LoRA in ComfyUI and kept the artefacts: *“The flicker between frames becomes a scar. The deformation becomes a declaration. The defect becomes the form.”* Secondary Alici analyses of dreamcore creators (rin_ai_cinematic, teber) likewise describe VHS filters / glitch transitions over Runway/Pika animation — treat those two as third-party analysis, not creator disclosure.
Sources: https://www.thedaringcreatives.com/creator-stories/the-dor-brothers-fifty-clips-keep-two/ ; https://digitalmedia-bremen.de/project/cuerpa-mostra/ — *index*.

**7. Uncanny faces — too many, misplaced, or swapped.**
Sora kept *adding* faces to shy kids’ balloon, plus strings and wrong colours; the team had to rotoscope/paint them out in post — evidence that face-wrongness is the models’ default failure mode, which this scene instead foregrounds (Kurupuri’s multi-eyed, multi-mouthed flesh faces; Runway 2026’s Grand Prix built entirely around a disfigured face; niceaunties’ *Mirror* replacing the viewer’s own face). Face-swap/borrowing (*Trick or Trend*) is the narrative version.
Sources: https://www.notebookcheck.net/OpenAI-s-Sora-finicky-to-work-with-needs-hundreds-of-prompts-serious-VFX-work-for-under-2-minutes-of-cohesive-story.831515.0.html ; https://www.stirworld.com/think-opinions-auntiescapes-at-load-gallery-asks-can-the-hyperreal-impact-social-reality — *index*.

**8. Impossible physics treated deadpan.**
Balloon head subject to ordinary gravity/embarrassment; Miyagi’s studio selling “transformation, time, and gravity” transcendence as its explicit brief; werewolf/human transformations in the wider TikTok trend layer (10-sec clips: eyes glow, fur/claws race up arms, face stretches to a muzzle — documented as a trend format, not a single auteur). The comedy/horror comes from bystanders and settings behaving normally.
Sources: https://openai.com/index/sora-first-impressions/ ; https://itbusinesstoday.com/tech/ai/aistudio-miyagi-debuts-creative-ai-video-platform/ — *index*.

**9. Rapid-fire scene turnover (≈1 second per world).**
Alici’s (secondary) breakdown of teber’s dreamcore montage counts **14 distinct scenes in 14 seconds** — pastel houses, a daisy-headed figure, floating clocks — at 106K likes. Combined with LoopHole’s 20-sec ceiling, the scene’s pacing norm is: a new impossible image before the previous one can be categorised.
Source: https://alici.ai/formulas/media/teber/469e7d72-b9a3-54ca-aab9-4c29c4564b1f-dreamcore-weirdcore-aesthetic-ai-video — *index, secondary analysis*.

**10. Caption-less / text-less opening.**
Kurupuri’s live feed posts carry essentially no explanatory caption; LoopHole rules actually *ban* opening titles, credits, logos or end cards inside submissions “to preserve continuity.” The image must do all the work in the first seconds — there is no verbal hook to hide behind.
Sources: https://x.com/kurupuri0 (*verified live*); https://www.theloopholeproject.ai/rules (*index*).

### 3. Workflow / tool signals — who publicly names what, for what effect

**The dominant pattern (confirmed across niceaunties, Boesch, Kinomura, Another World, Kapwing’s dreamcore tutorial): image first, then animate.** Build a still you are happy with (Midjourney / DALL·E 3 / Flux / Morphic), then image-to-video (Runway / Kling / Hailuo / Pika / Veo), then edit/music (Premiere/CapCut, Suno). Text-to-video alone is the exception (Trillo’s raw Sora tests, Dor Brothers’ later Seedance work).

### 4. Why grotesque / surreal / transformation imagery holds viewers — attention research & platform data

Read this as four separate, sourced mechanisms that *stack*. None of the lab studies used TikTok or AI video; the application to 10-second clips is an inference, labelled as such.

**A. Disgust holds attention earlier and longer than fear — at time-scales that match a scroll decision.**
- van Hooff et al. (2013, *Acta Psychologica* 143:1–6): in a covert-orienting task, only **disgust-evoking** images (not fear-evoking or neutral) delayed responses to targets presented **100–200 ms** after the image — i.e. disgust “temporarily grabbed hold” of attention, robustly and without voluntary attention instructions. Summary: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265692018_Disgust-specific_modulation_of_early_attention_processes — *index*.
- 2023 follow-up literature (PubMed record): images of disgusting creatures **facilitated attentional orienting and delayed attentional disengagement** vs. neutral images. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37750971/ — *index*.
- Implication for the producer: flesh/texture wrongness operates in the first *fifth of a second*, well before narrative or caption processing — which is why a caption-less first frame can work.

**B. Prediction error / scene incongruity: the wrong thing in a known place is fixated first and remembered best.**
- Allegretti & Coco (2023, eye-tracking, naturalistic scenes): objects **inconsistent with their scene** (their example: a torch vs. a toothbrush in a bathroom) were **fixated earlier and for longer** at encoding, and their changes were detected better and faster at recognition; the authors explicitly attribute this to a **prediction error in object-scene integration** that is “not yet resolved.” https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/1688968 — *index*. This is the closest lab analogue to “octopus in the office / flesh face at the desk.”
- Classic supporting result: Becker, Pashler & Lubin (2007, *J Exp Psychol: Human Perception & Performance* 33(1):20–30) — object-intrinsic oddities (e.g. a green stop sign) drew earlier saccades, from further away, than unaltered objects. Summarised at http://scienceblogs.com/cognitivedaily/2008/03/11/do-unusual-objects-attract-our/ — *index, secondary summary of a peer-reviewed paper*.
- Curiosity theory frames the unresolved part: Loewenstein’s (1994) **information-gap theory** — curiosity is a felt deprivation arising when attention focuses on a gap between what one knows and what one wants to know; Gottlieb et al. (2013, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*) review automatic attentional biases toward novelty/surprise alongside systematic information-seeking. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4193662/ — *index*. A morph that never completes, or a loop that resets before resolution, keeps the gap open.
- Dopamine prediction-error coding (Schultz, 2016 review, *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience*): dopamine neurons burst for outcomes **better/different than predicted**, dip for worse-than-predicted, and stay at baseline for fully predicted outcomes — “surprise directs learning/behaviour.” Fully predictable footage is, neurally, the baseline case. http://www.pdn.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/2016-schultz-dialoguesclinneurosci.pdf — *index*.

**C. Morbid curiosity is a measurable, reward-linked drive — not just shock tolerance.**
- Oosterwijk et al. (2020, *Scientific Reports* 10:15291, fMRI, preregistered): **choosing** to view intensely negative (morbid) images activated the **striatum, inferior frontal gyrus, anterior insula and anterior cingulate cortex** — “similar brain regions as those that support extrinsic incentives and ‘regular’ curiosity” — more than choosing positive images or passively viewing. The authors note negative information may be more novel, rare, deviant, uncertain and complex. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-71662-y — *index*.
- Scrivner (2021, *Personality and Individual Differences* 183:111139): Morbid Curiosity Scale development, Studies 1–2 n=1,370, Study 3 n=317, Study 4 n=137. Morbid curiosity **predicted over half the variance (r² = .53) in decisions to further investigate a morbid stimulus** (image/text choice task), and high scorers prefer threat-centred movies. Its domains explicitly include bodies and body violation. Full text: https://cdn2.psychologytoday.com/assets/pomc.pdf — *index*.
- Recreational-fear research adds the dose curve: Andersen et al. (2020, *Psychological Science*, field study, n=110 haunted-house visitors with heart-rate monitoring) found enjoyment had an **inverted-U relationship with fear** — too little is flat, too much is aversive, the middle is enjoyable — consistent with Rozin’s **benign masochism** (enjoying a negative sensation the body registers while the mind knows it is safe). This was read via secondary summaries, so treat the exact framing as *index/secondary*: https://humanumi.com/why-do-people-watch-horror-movies-the-answer-lies-in-the-psychology-of-fear/ . Practical read: 10-second, obviously-generated grotesque sits in the “safe” zone — threatening enough to hold, artificial enough not to trigger genuine avoidance for most viewers. Individual differences are large (Scrivner’s trait variance), so this will polarise — comments/reposts from the disgusted are still engagement.

**D. Platform data: the first 3 seconds are where distribution is decided — but the best-known figure is ad data.**
- TikTok for Business creative guidance, as widely reprinted: **“63% of all Auction Ads with the highest CTR highlight their key message or product in the first 3 seconds.”** Reprint with the original playbook wording: https://en.ppt-online.org/897175 — *index*. **Caveats (important):** this is (i) advertising, not organic, data; (ii) a description of winners, with no loser comparison group — a pattern, not causal proof. The vlix analysis makes exactly this caveat while citing it: https://www.vlix.me/digital-marketing/what-makes-a-good-hook-for-tiktok-and-youtube-shorts — *index*.
- The same vlix piece cites a more direct behavioural measurement from a short-video streaming study: **15,344 swipes recorded from 133 participants** on a real feed; **29% of swipes landed in the first fifth of a video** and 42% in the last fifth (mostly natural endings), with only 6% between 60–80% through — i.e. surviving the opening disproportionately predicts surviving to the middle. It also cites Meta’s often-quoted **1.7 seconds average dwell** on a piece of mobile-feed content, while noting Meta has not published the method. Treat both as *index/secondary*.
- No platform (TikTok, Meta, YouTube) was found publishing a retention breakdown *by visual content type* (grotesque vs. neutral). Anyone claiming “horror hooks retain X% better on TikTok” without a primary dataset is extrapolating — including this report’s synthesis in A–C.

**Synthesis for a 10-second clip / music video (inference, clearly labelled):** put a scene-inconsistent, disgust-textured transformation *in frame 1* (mechanisms A+B), keep it *unresolved* — mid-morph, looping, or cutting to a second incongruity at ~1–3 sec (mechanism B, information gap), and keep the threat *obviously artificial / playful enough* to stay on the enjoyable side of the recreational-fear curve (mechanism C). That is precisely the form Kurupuri, LoopHole, Trillo and the dreamcore creators have converged on, apparently independently of the lab literature.
