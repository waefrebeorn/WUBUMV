# WINNERS CANON — the reference shelf

The award winners and all-time-list videos this studio treats as canon, each
with its SIGNATURE EVENT — the happening it's remembered for (the unit the
EVENT_LIBRARY trades in). Compiled 2026-11-13; research + sources in
`~/workspace/research_notes/mv-event-library-2026-11-13/report.md` (Stream A).
Signature events are the remembered happenings as documented in the cited
coverage; craft-category credits are as reported by those sources.

## MTV VMA — Video of the Year (selected, event-bearing)

| Year | Artist — Title | Director | Signature event |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1984 | The Cars — "You Might Think" | Jeff Stein / Alex Weil | Early CGI: the singer's face and body composited into cartoons and household objects — the performer inside the graphics |
| 1985 | Don Henley — "The Boys of Summer" | Jean-Baptiste Mondino | A drummer plays on a beach as the tide and dusk take the scene — performance inside a decaying postcard world |
| 1986 | Dire Straits — "Money for Nothing" | Steve Barron | Blocky CGI movers watch TV inside the video; lyric illustrated literally by computer-animated workers |
| 1987 | Peter Gabriel — "Sledgehammer" | Stephen R. Johnson | Stop-motion invasion of a man's head: dancing chickens, fruit faces, clay worlds — a new micro-event every few seconds (also won Direction, Special Effects, Editing, Art Direction, Cinematography — 9 VMAs, the record) |
| 1989 | Neil Young — "This Note's for You" | Julien Temple | The singer performs inside parodies of the era's own ad-style videos — the medium mocking itself |
| 1990 | Sinéad O'Connor — "Nothing Compares 2 U" | John Maybury | One held close-up; a single real tear falls — the micro-event |
| 1991 | R.E.M. — "Losing My Religion" | Tarsem Singh | Religious tableaux (winged old man, bound saint) pose, then come alive |
| 1992 | Van Halen — "Right Now" | Mark Fenske | Captions stating plain truths roll over everyday scenes — text as commentary event (post-layer ancestor) |
| 1995 | TLC — "Waterfalls" | F. Gary Gray | The singers stand on water; mini-dramas play out at the shoreline |
| 1996 | Alanis Morissette — "Ironic" | Stéphane Sednaoui | Four versions of the singer ride in one car, each performing a different verse mood — multiplication by casting |
| 1997 | Jamiroquai — "Virtual Insanity" | Jonathan Glazer | The room dances: floor and furniture glide under a centred singer |
| 1999 | Lauryn Hill — "Doo Wop (That Thing)" | Big TV! | Split-screen: the same block party staged in 1967 and 1998 simultaneously |
| 2000 | Eminem — "The Real Slim Shady" | Dr. Dre / Phillip Atwell | A factory clones the singer; hundreds of identical doubles fill frames |
| 2002 | Eminem — "Without Me" | Joseph Kahn | The singer as a superhero sidekick in a comic-book world that keeps interrupting itself |
| 2005 | Green Day — "Boulevard of Broken Dreams" | Samuel Bayer | The band walks away from a broken-down car through a desaturated desert world |
| 2007 | Rihanna ft. Jay-Z — "Umbrella" | Chris Applebaum | The singer dances in rain that falls in silver sparks; body painted silver in a triangle of light |
| 2009 | Beyoncé — "Single Ladies" | Jake Nava | One take, white void, three dancers: the hand-flip routine becomes the decade's most-copied move |
| 2010 | Lady Gaga — "Bad Romance" | Francis Lawrence | A bath-house of pods: the singer is hatched, displayed, and sold in a white latex world |
| 2016 | Beyoncé — "Formation" | Melina Matsoukas | The singer sits on a police car sinking into floodwater; troupe formations in house and lot |
| 2017 | Kendrick Lamar — "HUMBLE." | Dave Meyers / The Little Homies | Tableaux barrage: head on fire at dinner, Last-Supper lineup, grey heads passing a jar hand to hand |
| 2020 | The Weeknd — "Blinding Lights" | Anton Tammi | A bloodied singer staggers and dances through neon night streets, grinning — one escalating night-walk |
| 2021 | Lil Nas X — "Montero (Call Me By Your Name)" | Tanu Muino / Lil Nas X | The singer pole-dances down from heaven into hell — the descent as the event |
| 2023 | Taylor Swift — "Anti-Hero" | Taylor Swift | The singer's chaotic double crashes her own scenes; a giant version of her peers into a dollhouse dinner |
| 2024 | Taylor Swift ft. Post Malone — "Fortnight" | Taylor Swift | A typewriter-office asylum world; papers fly, the desk becomes the stage |
| 2025 | Ariana Grande — "Brighter Days Ahead" | Christian Breslauer | Long-form: the singer revisits her own past videos as an old woman — self-canon as the event |

Sources: Capital FM VMA winners list (capitalfm.com/events/mtv-vmas/news/video-of-the-year-award-winners/); 2025 winners via Soap Central / Hollywood Reporter coverage (soapcentral.com/music/2025-mtv-vmas-complete-list-winners-and-nominees).
