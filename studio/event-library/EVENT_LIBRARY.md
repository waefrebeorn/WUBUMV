# EVENT LIBRARY — the WuBuMV studio catalog of things that HAPPEN

**What this is.** A working catalog of on-screen EVENTS for music-video scene
planning. An *event* is a happening inside one scene with a start → peak →
payoff — the thing a viewer can describe afterwards in one sentence. This is a
different axis from the visual-mv-forms skill (forms are containers: kinetic
type, montage, visualizer). A form says *what kind of slot it is*; an event
says *what happens in it*. Bland videos come from form-rich, event-poor plans:
scenes that are subjects at places, singing, while nothing happens.

**How to use it.** At scene-plan time every slot gets ONE event from this
library, written into the vehicle as the scene's verb before any other prompt
detail. Full procedure: `WORKFLOW.md` in this directory. Canon sources:
`WINNERS_CANON.md`. What's already spent: `USED_EVENTS.md`.

**Fields per entry.** *Energy* = Low / Mid / High (first-3-seconds rule: intro
slots need High). *Slots* = where it earns its keep. *Feasibility* = against
our generator (one ~5–10 s generated scene, 16:9, adults only, originals only,
no generated text — type is post). *Repeat-risk* = how fast it stales.

---

## Family 1 — TRANSFORMATION (a thing becomes another thing)

**1.1 The Morph Chain.** Start: a face/object at rest. Peak: it melts/morphs
through two or three forms. Payoff: it lands as a new face/object that holds
for the last beat. *Canon:* "Black or White" face-morph chain (MJ, 1991);
"Hyperballad" avatar-over-body (Björk, 1996). *Energy:* High. *Slots:* intro
grab, bridge turn, tail button. *Feasibility:* native — use the two-image
morph discipline (stage the morph as the transition, cut on the landing).
*Repeat-risk:* High — one morph per video, and not in back-to-back MVs.

**1.2 Medium Swap.** Start: the world in its normal medium. Peak: the world
visibly converts — to sketch lines, toy blocks, clay, paper cut-out. Payoff:
the scene plays out in the new medium for its last beats. *Canon:* "Take On
Me" sketch world (a-ha, 1985); "Fell in Love with a Girl" LEGO world (White
Stripes, 2002); "Big Time" clay world (Peter Gabriel, 1986). *Energy:* High.
*Slots:* chorus lift, bridge, section change. *Feasibility:* stage via ref
stacks in the target medium; true stop-motion judder is post, never prompted.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium — the *target* medium must differ each use.

**1.3 Costume-Peel Escalation.** Start: performer in layer one. Peak: they
peel/tear away a layer to reveal a wilder costume beneath — twice, escalating.
Payoff: final reveal is the most extreme (skeleton *costume*, full glitter
suit, mascot head). *Canon:* "Rock DJ" skin-peel (Robbie Williams, 2000 —
restaged here as costume, no gore). *Energy:* High. *Slots:* chorus, hook.
*Feasibility:* good in anime; each layer is a ref-still state. *Repeat-risk:*
Medium — escalation order must change.

**1.4 The Prop Comes Alive.** Start: an ordinary prop sits still. Peak: it
sprouts limbs/a face and performs one action (dances, points, plays itself).
Payoff: it freezes back into an ordinary prop as if nothing happened.
*Canon:* "Sledgehammer" dancing chickens/fruit faces (1986); "Money for
Nothing" TV-world objects (1985). *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* verse colour,
montage cover, hook punctuation. *Feasibility:* generator-native; one action
only, keep it simple. *Repeat-risk:* Low — different prop = new event.

**1.5 Performer Becomes Graphic.** Start: live performer mid-move. Peak: the
body flattens into a poster/logo/halftone version of itself. Payoff: the
graphic version strikes the pose the live body couldn't hold. *Canon:*
"Californication" game-avatar band (RHCP, 2000); "Hyperballad" game sprite
(1996). *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* bridge, rap/fast sections, montage cover.
*Feasibility:* ref-stack style state + post grade; body must be full-frame.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**1.6 Elemental Body.** Start: a figure made of water/leaves/smoke/sparks
holds human shape. Peak: it performs (dances, reaches) while shedding its
element. Payoff: it disperses on the last beat — or re-forms. *Canon:*
inspired by "Sledgehammer"'s material-play (1986) and elemental figures
across Björk's Gondry videos. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* bridge, outro, intro.
*Feasibility:* generator does particle-bodies unevenly — keep the figure
large, the move simple. *Repeat-risk:* High — signature-tier, ration it.

## Family 2 — SCALE (the wrong size for the world)

**2.1 Giant in a Small World.** Start: normal street/room. Peak: the performer
is suddenly building-tall (or table-tall among normal people), interacting
carefully with tiny things. Payoff: a delicate interaction lands (steps over a
bus, picks up a car like a toy). *Canon:* "Big Time" scale gags (1986);
"Lost!" oversized office world (RM, 2024). *Energy:* High. *Slots:* intro,
chorus. *Feasibility:* good; lock camera low for the giant read. *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

**2.2 Shrink to the Desktop World.** Start: performer normal. Peak: they are
suddenly palm-sized on a desk/tabletop among giant everyday objects. Payoff:
they use a giant object as landscape (climb a mug, surf a spoon). *Canon:*
"Big Time" (1986) miniature passages. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* verse journey,
bridge. *Feasibility:* good; giant props must be named, simple shapes.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**2.3 The Wrong-Size Prop.** Start: an everyday object appears — absurdly
large (a phone the size of a door) or tiny (a piano that fits a palm). Peak:
the performer uses it *as if normal*. Payoff: a second character reacts (or
pointedly doesn't). *Canon:* "Lost!" (2024) scale-wrong workplace sets.
*Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* verse, visual pun support, montage cover.
*Feasibility:* excellent — one prop, deadpan use. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**2.4 The Room Shrinks.** Start: a normal room with the performer inside.
Peak: walls/ceiling visibly close in across the scene. Payoff: the performer
ends nose-to-wall, still singing/performing, unbothered. *Canon:* "Virtual
Insanity"'s moving-room grammar, inverted (Jamiroquai, 1996). *Energy:* Mid.
*Slots:* bridge, tension verses. *Feasibility:* moderate — slow continuous
set move is generator-friendly. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**2.5 Head-Growth Gag.** Start: close-up, normal proportions. Peak: the head
(or hair, or hat) grows across the scene, cartoon-style. Payoff: it pops back
to normal on the cut/beat, or fills the frame. *Canon:* "Big Time" clay
head-growth (1986). *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* hook punctuation, comedy
verses. *Feasibility:* anime only; live-action reads as error. *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

## Family 3 — GRAVITY & PHYSICS (the world stops obeying)

**3.1 Deadpan Flight.** Start: a grounded, ordinary moment (a lobby, a
sidewalk). Peak: the performer calmly leaves the ground and floats/flies a
short arc, expression unchanged. Payoff: a gentle landing, business as usual.
*Canon:* "Weapon of Choice" lobby flight (Fatboy Slim, 2001). *Energy:* High.
*Slots:* intro grab, chorus peak. *Feasibility:* stage the mid-flight span;
take-off/landing are the weak frames — start already rising. *Repeat-risk:*
High — the canon owns this one; re-skin the setting hard.

**3.2 The Floor Drops.** Start: solid ground, mid-action. Peak: the floor
falls away / a trapdoor opens / the ground becomes water. Payoff: the
performer adapts instantly (swims, lands in a boat, keeps dancing on a raft).
*Canon:* "Virtual Insanity" floor grammar (1996); "Formation" sinking tableau
(2016). *Energy:* High. *Slots:* section changes, bridge entry. *Feasibility:*
good; one clean drop, no complex falling choreography. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**3.3 Reverse-Time Rebuild.** Start: a mess mid-scene — spilled drink, broken
object, scattered papers. Peak: everything runs backwards: spill climbs into
the glass, shards reassemble, papers stack themselves. Payoff: pristine scene,
performer smug. *Canon:* "Sugar Water" forward/backward split (Cibo Matto,
1996) — the time-reversal half. *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* bridge, verse
turn. *Feasibility:* prompt the reverse action directly (objects rising,
reassembling); assembly can also flip a forward take. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**3.4 Room Zero-G.** Start: a furnished room, gravity normal. Peak: objects
lift and drift; the performer pushes off a wall and glides. Payoff: everything
settles back down at once on the final beat. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* dream/
bridge sections, instrumental. *Feasibility:* moderate — drifting props read
better than drifting people; keep human motion to one glide. *Repeat-risk:*
High.

**3.5 Gravity Flip.** Start: normal orientation. Peak: on a beat, "down"
changes — performer lands on the ceiling and stands there. Payoff: they
continue the scene upside-down, furniture raining up past them. *Energy:*
High. *Slots:* hook, bridge turn. *Feasibility:* generate the ceiling-world
directly (inverted set in the ref), don't flip footage with people mid-move.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**3.6 The Impossible Object.** Start: one object behaves wrongly — a stone
skips across water forever, a ball bounces *up* stairs, a coin spins for the
whole scene. Peak: the wrongness escalates (the stone skips over a boat).
Payoff: a character finally catches/steps on it and normality resumes.
*Canon:* "Skipping Like a Stone" (Chemical Brothers, 2024, UKMVA Best VFX).
*Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* instrumental, intro, montage cover. *Feasibility:*
excellent — object-led, no acting needed. *Repeat-risk:* Low — swap the
object, it's new.

## Family 4 — MULTIPLICATION (copies arrive)

**4.1 Clone Handoff.** Start: performer alone with an object. Peak: copies of
the performer file in and pass the object hand-to-hand down a line. Payoff:
the last copy hands it back to the first — loop closed. *Canon:* "HUMBLE."
grey-heads jar handoff (Kendrick Lamar, 2017); "The Real Slim Shady" clone
factory (Eminem, 2000). *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* chorus, counted/list
lyrics. *Feasibility:* 3–5 copies max in one frame; identical outfit locked by
ref. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**4.2 The Swarm.** Start: one small thing (bird, bee, paper plane, spark).
Peak: dozens pour in, filling the sky/room in a moving pattern. Payoff: they
form a shape (arrow, heart, the carry object) for one beat, then scatter.
*Energy:* High. *Slots:* chorus lift, intro. *Feasibility:* swarms are
generator-friendly (no faces); keep the formed shape simple and large.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium — the formed shape must change per use.

**4.3 Mirror Army.** Start: performer faces a mirror/window. Peak: the
reflection steps out; then every reflective surface produces another.
Payoff: a synchronized line of selves strikes one pose. *Energy:* Mid.
*Slots:* bridge, vanity/ego lyrics. *Feasibility:* moderate; reflections are
a known generator weakness — stage copies as physical doubles, not literal
mirror output. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**4.4 Cast Assembly.** Start: an empty table/doorway/vehicle. Peak: the cast
arrives ONE by one, each entrance a mini-event (drops from above, slides in,
bursts through a door). Payoff: the full group poses/acts together for the
first time. *Canon:* "360" dinner-table assembly (Charli XCX, 2024, UKMVA
Video of the Year) — restaged with original casts only. *Energy:* Mid–High.
*Slots:* intro (meet the cast), final chorus. *Feasibility:* one arrival per
scene; assembly across cuts for larger casts. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**4.5 Dance With Your Double.** Start: performer begins a simple move. Peak:
an identical double slides in and mirrors/answers the moves. Payoff: the two
swap places so smoothly you lose track of the original. *Canon:* "Single
Ladies" trio grammar reduced to two (Beyoncé, 2008). *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:*
chorus, duet-feel sections. *Feasibility:* simple unison moves only.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

## Family 5 — OBJECT REBELLION (props act on their own)

**5.1 The Chain Reaction.** Start: a finger flicks a domino / a ball rolls.
Peak: a Rube-Goldberg chain crosses the whole set — ramps, pulleys, falling
books, a fan, a rolling egg. Payoff: the chain's final action lands ON the
lyric's key word (a flag pops, a photo flips, a bell rings). *Canon:* "This
Too Shall Pass" (OK Go, 2010). *Energy:* High. *Slots:* intro, instrumental,
hook. *Feasibility:* ONE chain leg per scene (see report C.2); assembly chains
legs. *Repeat-risk:* Low — every chain is bespoke.

**5.2 Prop Cascade.** Start: one object tips. Peak: the tip cascades — shelves
of cans, a row of umbrellas, stacked chairs fall in a wave across the frame.
Payoff: the last object lands upright, impossibly neat. *Energy:* Mid–High.
*Slots:* chorus punctuation, montage cover. *Feasibility:* excellent;
cascades are single-cause motion. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**5.3 Appliance Choreography.** Start: a kitchen/laundromat/office at rest.
Peak: the machines perform — washer doors flap in rhythm, lamps nod, chairs
spin in unison. Payoff: everything stops dead when a human looks. *Canon:*
"Sledgehammer" object-life (1986); Gondry's animated object worlds.
*Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* verse colour, comedy slots. *Feasibility:* good;
machines have no faces to break. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**5.4 The Runaway Object.** Start: an object escapes (hat in wind, rolling
suitcase, bouncing ball). Peak: a chase — the object stays one step ahead,
taking corners and stairs. Payoff: caught at last — or it turns and chases
the character. *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* verse journey, montage spine.
*Feasibility:* good; chase = simple directional motion. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**5.5 They Build It Themselves.** Start: scattered parts on a table/floor.
Peak: unheld, the parts slide, lift, and assemble themselves into the finished
thing. Payoff: the finished object switches on / opens / glows. *Energy:*
Mid. *Slots:* intro (build the world), bridge. *Feasibility:* good — reverse-
time trick also available in assembly (film disassembly, flip it).
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**5.6 The Answering World (prop scale).** Start: the performer touches/steps/
knocks. Peak: the world answers — floor tiles light under each step, a wall
ripples from a touch, a table of objects jumps at a clap. Payoff: the answers
build a pattern that completes on the downbeat. *Canon:* "Billie Jean"
light-up pavement (MJ, 1983). *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* hook, chorus,
journey. *Feasibility:* excellent; the response is local and simple.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium — the answering surface must change.

## Family 6 — WORLD EVENTS (the set itself acts)

**6.1 The Moving Floor.** Start: a furnished room, performer centred. Peak:
floor and furniture glide — sofas slide past, walls breathe in and out — while
the performer dances in place, eerily stable. Payoff: the room parks itself in
a new arrangement. *Canon:* "Virtual Insanity" (Jamiroquai, 1996). *Energy:*
High. *Slots:* verse spine, chorus. *Feasibility:* moderate — prompt the SET
moving, performer simple. *Repeat-risk:* High — canon-identified; vary the
room and the glide pattern.

**6.2 The Set Rebuilds.** Start: an empty/bare space. Peak: walls, windows,
furniture fly/slide in and assemble a complete new room around the performer.
Payoff: the performer sits/stands in the finished room as if it always was.
*Energy:* High. *Slots:* section change, intro world-build. *Feasibility:*
good in anime; pieces arrive in 2–3 waves, not all at once. *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

**6.3 Season Flip.** Start: one landscape/street in one season. Peak: mid-
scene the season turns — snow melts to blossom, autumn leaves flood in, rain
dries to harsh summer light. Payoff: the performer, unchanged, in the new
season. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* bridge, time-passing verses. *Feasibility:*
good; anchor on one landmark that survives the flip. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**6.4 The Water Arrives.** Start: a dry street/room. Peak: water rises —
puddles merge, a tide rolls in, the floor becomes a shallow sea. Payoff:
everyone/thing is ankle-deep, reflections everywhere, life continues adapted
(boots, a boat, dancing in the flood). *Canon:* "Formation" floodwater
tableau (2016), staged as arrival. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* chorus change,
bridge. *Feasibility:* good; keep it shallow and slow. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**6.5 Chaos Behind, Calm In Front.** Start: performer grooves/walks toward
camera, pleasant and unbothered. Peak: behind them, the world erupts — a
parade crashes, furniture flies, a crowd brawls with confetti. Payoff: the
performer finally glances back; everything instantly poses innocent.
*Canon:* "This Is America" (Childish Gambino, 2018) — restaged playful, no
violence. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* chorus, hook. *Feasibility:* moderate —
background events must be BIG simple motions (no fine acting). *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

**6.6 The World Rotates.** Start: a street/room scene. Peak: the entire world
slowly rotates around the performer (or the horizon tilts 90°), gravity
apparently holding them in place. Payoff: the world parks sideways; the
performer shrugs and continues. *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* bridge, dream
sections. *Feasibility:* moderate; slow rotation only. *Repeat-risk:* High.

## Family 7 — WEATHER & LIGHT HITS (the sky lands on a beat)

**7.1 Storm on the Downbeat.** Start: calm, bright. Peak: on the section's
first downbeat a storm front visibly rolls in — clouds stack, light dies,
first rain sheet crosses. Payoff: the scene continues in storm-light, a new
world for the price of one hit. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* bridge entry, final
chorus darkening. *Feasibility:* excellent (sky is easy, no faces).
*Repeat-risk:* Medium — must not become the default bridge move.

**7.2 The Blackout Snap.** Start: full bright scene. Peak: every light dies
on one frame except ONE practical (a lamp, a phone, a fridge door). Payoff:
the scene plays in single-source light, or the lights slam back on the next
hit. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* hook punctuation, pre-chorus tension.
*Feasibility:* excellent; also a post-grade trick if generation resists.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**7.3 Day–Night Flip.** Start: midday scene. Peak: the sun races across the
sky; shadows sweep; night lands with windows lighting up. Payoff: the same
action continues under night light, unbroken. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:*
time-passage verses, outro. *Feasibility:* good; keep the subject's action
continuous and simple. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**7.4 Personal Weather.** Start: a sunny crowd/street. Peak: one small rain
cloud (or snow flurry, or sunbeam) follows exactly one character. Payoff:
they accept it — umbrella up, still dancing — while everyone else stays dry.
*Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* comedy verses, character intros. *Feasibility:*
good; cloud small and high-contrast. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

## Family 8 — SNAP CHANGES (one frame, everything's different)

**8.1 Pose-Strike Chain.** Start: performer mid-motion. Peak: on each beat
they freeze into a bold editorial pose — silhouette-first, held — while the
WORLD changes around the pose. Payoff: the last pose is the biggest and holds
into the next section. *Canon:* "Vogue" pose grammar (Madonna, 1990); house
POSE-CUT device. *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* section boundaries, chorus.
*Feasibility:* excellent — held poses are generator-safe. *Repeat-risk:* Low–
Medium (house device; the poses must be new).

**8.2 Beat Costume Snap.** Start: outfit A. Peak: on a named hit the outfit is
simply outfit B — no transition, mid-move. Payoff: a third snap lands the
hero outfit for the payoff line. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* chorus, hook.
*Feasibility:* excellent via per-state refs; mid-motion continuity is loose
by design. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**8.3 Palette Snap.** Start: the world in palette A. Peak: on the hit, the
whole world's colour inverts/swaps to palette B (sky, clothes, props).
Payoff: characters notice their own changed colours. *Energy:* Mid–High.
*Slots:* section change, LIGHT-SHIFT sections. *Feasibility:* moderate in
generation; bulletproof as a post grade swap if the palette is hue-based.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**8.4 Location Snap, Pose Held.** Start: performer mid-pose/action in place A.
Peak: the place snaps to place B (same pose, same framing) — then C. Payoff:
the final place is the one the lyric names. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* journey
verses, list lyrics. *Feasibility:* excellent — the pose continuity sells it;
places are separate plates. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**8.5 The Prop Swap.** Start: performer holding object A, using it. Peak:
across two or three cuts the object in their hand is suddenly B, then C —
each used as naturally as the last. Payoff: the final object is the joke/
payoff (a microphone becomes a banana becomes a trophy). *Energy:* Mid.
*Slots:* list lyrics, comedy. *Feasibility:* excellent. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

## Family 9 — CROWD EVENTS (the many move as one)

**9.1 The Freeze.** Start: a busy crowd scene, everyone moving. Peak: on a
beat the ENTIRE crowd freezes mid-gesture — only the performer still moves.
Payoff: the performer weaves through the statues; the crowd unfreezes on the
next hit. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* intro grab, chorus. *Feasibility:* good —
crowd as background layer with a state change (house H3 grammar).
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**9.2 Scatter & Reform.** Start: an ordered crowd (queue, formation, class).
Peak: something startles them — they scatter in all directions. Payoff: they
reform into a NEW formation (a shape, a line, a letter) for the payoff beat.
*Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* chorus, hook. *Feasibility:* moderate; scatter
direction simple/radial. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**9.3 The Turn.** Start: a crowd with its back to camera / attention
elsewhere. Peak: on one frame every head turns to the same point (the
performer, the sky, the door). Payoff: total stillness, all eyes one way.
*Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* reveals, intro of a character. *Feasibility:*
backs-to-camera staging is house-safe (MV-14 precedent). *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

**9.4 Join-In Wave.** Start: performer dances alone; bystanders watch. Peak:
one bystander joins, then two, then the whole frame is dancing the same
simple move. Payoff: the original performer gets lifted/absorbed into the
group's unison. *Canon:* "It's Oh So Quiet" street eruption (Björk, 1995);
"Praise You" public routine (1999). *Energy:* High. *Slots:* final chorus,
outro. *Feasibility:* simple unison move only; join across cuts. *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

**9.5 The Parting.** Start: a dense crowd fills the frame, backs to camera.
Peak: the crowd parts down the middle like water. Payoff: the revealed thing
(the performer, the vehicle, the carry object) stands in the cleared lane,
lit. *Energy:* High. *Slots:* intro reveal, final chorus entrance.
*Feasibility:* good; symmetric parting is a clean prompt. *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

## Family 10 — BODY & POSE EVENTS (the body does the impossible or the singular)

**10.1 The Signature Move.** Start: ordinary dancing/movement. Peak: the
performer unveils ONE invented move — a specific, nameable gesture (the
hand-flip-and-hip, the shoulder-roll-into-spin) — and repeats it bigger.
Payoff: everyone in frame is doing it by the scene's end. *Canon:* "Single
Ladies" hand-flip routine (2008). *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:* chorus (this
is what choruses are for). *Feasibility:* the move must be ONE simple joint
action; complex choreography fails. *Repeat-risk:* Low — a new move per
video is the point.

**10.2 Impossible Bend.** Start: normal stance. Peak: the body bends/stretches
beyond anatomy — a backbend to the floor, an arm that reaches too far, a neck
turn too smooth. Payoff: snapped back to normal, deadpan. *Canon:* "The Rain
(Supa Dupa Fly)" body-distortion grammar (Missy Elliott, 1997) — restaged as
bend, not lens. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* weird verses, bridges. *Feasibility:*
anime yes; live-action reads as glitch — use deliberately or not at all.
*Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**10.3 The Tableau Wakes.** Start: a frozen group scene posed like a painting
(religious/classical/fashion tableau). Peak: one figure moves — then all of
them, breaking the pose into action. Payoff: the tableau's story plays out for
two beats before the cut. *Canon:* "Losing My Religion" living tableaux
(R.E.M., 1991). *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* bridge, ballad sections.
*Feasibility:* excellent — starts from a held frame, motion law satisfied by
the wake. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**10.4 The Unstoppable Walk.** Start: the performer walking toward camera (or
through a world). Peak: obstacles, crowds, weather, doors — nothing slows or
diverts them; they pass through the scene's whole geography. Payoff: they
arrive exactly on the section's end, still walking. *Canon:* "Bitter Sweet
Symphony" (The Verve, 1997); "Unfinished Sympathy" (Massive Attack, 1991).
*Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* verse spine, journey sections. *Feasibility:*
excellent; walk cycles are safe. *Repeat-risk:* Medium — geography must
change.

**10.5 The Micro-Event.** Start: an unbroken close-up, nothing else moving.
Peak: ONE tiny thing happens — a tear falls, a smile cracks, a blink pattern
spells the beat. Payoff: the cut finally releases the held frame. *Canon:*
"Nothing Compares 2 U" tear (Sinéad O'Connor, 1990). *Energy:* Low in motion,
High in weight. *Slots:* the stillest bridge moment, once. *Feasibility:*
GAMBLE — flagged in report C.3: strongest close-up pipeline only, montage
cover ready, at most once per catalogue. *Repeat-risk:* Very high — ration.

## Family 11 — VISUAL PUNS (the lyric, made literal, for one beat)

**11.1 The Named Object Acts.** Start: the lyric names a thing (a train, a
lemon, a telephone). Peak: exactly as named, the thing enters and does the
verb the lyric gives it. Payoff: it's gone by the next line, never explained.
*Canon:* "Money for Nothing"'s literal lyric illustration (Dire Straits,
1985). *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* any lyric with a concrete noun+verb (the word
grid finds them). *Feasibility:* excellent. *Repeat-risk:* Very low — the
lyric writes a new one every song.

**11.2 Idiom Acted Out.** Start: an idiom line approaches ("heart on my
sleeve", "head in the clouds"). Peak: staged literally — an actual heart
pinned on a sleeve; the singer's head literally in a cloud layer. Payoff:
deadpan continuation. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* hook lines, title lines.
*Feasibility:* excellent in anime. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**11.3 The Number Made Real.** Start: the lyric counts or numbers something.
Peak: exactly that many objects/characters appear and are visibly countable.
Payoff: the count completes ON the naming word (never after). *Canon:*
house D1 counter grammar; "Around the World"'s counted tribes (Daft Punk,
1997). *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* list/count lyrics, hooks. *Feasibility:*
excellent; keep counts ≤7 for legibility. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

**11.4 Weather Pun.** Start: the lyric mentions rain/sun/fire figuratively.
Peak: the figure becomes real for one beat — it actually rains on the word
"rain", the room actually warms on "fire". Payoff: instant return to normal.
*Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* hook words, chorus keywords. *Feasibility:*
excellent (weather hits are easy). *Repeat-risk:* Low–Medium.

**11.5 The Door Reveal.** Start: a door/window/curtain in frame, closed.
Peak: it opens on the lyric's reveal word — behind it is the LAST thing
expected (the sea inside a closet, the singer inside their own fridge).
Payoff: the door closes; nobody comments. *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:*
verse turns, pre-chorus. *Feasibility:* good; the behind-door world is a
separate plate/state. *Repeat-risk:* Low.

## Family 12 — GRAPHIC EVENTS (the post layer acts)

*All Family 12 events are produced in POST (ASS / PNG / code layers) over
generated plates, per house law — the plate's job is to offer the surface,
the hand, the blank. The generated scene never carries text.*

**12.1 The Card Flip.** Start: the performer holds a stack of BLANK cards/
placards. Peak: they flip/drop cards in time with the lyric — post puts each
word on its card as it lands face-up. Payoff: the last card is dropped and
the performer walks off. *Canon:* "Subterranean Homesick Blues" cue cards
(Dylan, 1965). *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* fast verses, rap, lists.
*Feasibility:* cards generated blank, face-up holds ≥12 frames for the post
word to sit. *Repeat-risk:* High — Dylan owns it; use rarely, re-skin hard.

**12.2 Type Lands on the World.** Start: a plate with a declared landing
surface (wall, road, tabletop). Peak: the lyric's key word FALLS/slides in
(post kinetic) and settles onto the surface with weight — shadow, settle,
dust-puff in the plate if staged. Payoff: the word stays as set dressing for
the rest of the scene. *Canon:* "The Child" type-architecture (Alex Gopher,
1999), miniaturized to one word. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* hook words, title
moments. *Feasibility:* post-native; plate must declare the empty surface in
the ref brief. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**12.3 Worn Graphics.** Start: performer in a BLANK shirt / holding a blank
sign / a blank billboard behind. Peak: post graphics animate ON the blank —
the design draws itself, changes with the lyric, reacts to the action.
Payoff: the final design freezes as the scene's souvenir image. *Canon:*
"D.A.N.C.E." animated T-shirts (Justice, 2007). *Energy:* Mid–High. *Slots:*
chorus, hook, character intros. *Feasibility:* blanks must be big, front-on,
and tracked-friendly (minimal torso twist in the plate). *Repeat-risk:*
Medium.

**12.4 Interface Takeover.** Start: the scene framed as a familiar interface
— phone chat, game HUD, camera viewfinder (post frame). Peak: the interface
misbehaves as an event: messages arrive from the wrong person, the game
score counts the lyric, the viewfinder's focus hunts the wrong face. Payoff:
the interface glitches out and the plain scene resumes. *Canon:* "Roar"
texting lyric video (Katy Perry, 2013); "Californication" game frame (2000).
*Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* bridges, phone/text lyrics, rap sections.
*Feasibility:* post-native (house D2 HUD family). *Repeat-risk:* Medium —
one interface per video (report B.2 rule 4).

**12.5 The Ident Event.** Start: black or the carry colour. Peak: a fake
vintage production-logo spectacle performs in post — chrome letters fly,
lasers sweep, a star wipes — spelling the section's title/hook word. Payoff:
it resolves into a "logo" that stamps the section like a TV ident. *Canon:*
"DVNO" logo-ident lyrics (Justice, 2008). *Energy:* High for its 3–5 s.
*Slots:* intro title, section bumpers. *Feasibility:* post/code render;
Family G proof gate applies if code-rendered. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.

**12.6 The Word Chase.** Start: post letters of a key word scattered across
the plate. Peak: the letters flee the performer / chase each other / get
herded across surfaces in time with the phrase. Payoff: they assemble into
the word exactly on its sung onset, in the declared empty area. *Canon:*
"The Child" (1999); "Want It Back" calligraphy contact (Amanda Palmer) for
the on-body variant. *Energy:* Mid. *Slots:* fast phrases, hooks.
*Feasibility:* post-native kinetic work; budget it — this is the most
labour-intensive graphic event. *Repeat-risk:* Medium.
